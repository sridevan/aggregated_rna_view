# Scientific review: overlapping BGSU hairpin and junction loop annotations at RF00167 consensus 53

Date: 2026-10-16. Follows `REPORT.md` / `audit.py`. No data, classification, mapping or aggregation was changed. Sources: BGSU loop records (`annotation_assessment/cache/bgsu/loops_*.csv`), BGSU loop pages (`cache/loop_view_HL_1Y26_002.html`, `cache/loop_view_J3_1Y26_001.html`), FR3D base-pair annotations served by PDBe (`cache/fr3d_*_basepair.json`, fields `3d_id` = author number, `seq_id` = label_seq_id), and Petrov, Zirbel & Leontis (2013) *RNA* 19:1327, "Automated classification of RNA 3D motifs and the RNA 3D Motif Atlas" ([PMC3854523](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3854523/), [journal](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/19/10/1327)).

## 1. 1Y26 chain X: exact overlap

| Loop | BGSU record | Nucleotides (author) | Consensus |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HL_1Y26_002` (HL_56046.1 "Purine riboswitch") | sequence GUUUCUAC, 8 nt, bulged base U48, QA "Valid loop", base-pair signature `cWW-F-F-F-F-F` | G46 U47 U48 U49 C50 U51 A52 C53 | 50–57 |
| `J3_1Y26_001` (J3_44163.4) | sequence AUAAU\*AGUUUCUACC\*GAUU, 19 nt, bulged base U48, QA "Valid loop", signature `cWW-cWS-cWW-F-tWS-F-F-cWW-cWW-cWW-F-F-F` | A21–U25 · A45–C54 · G72–U75 | 25–29 · 49–58 · 78–81 |

- **Shared nucleotides:** all eight hairpin nucleotides G46–C53 (consensus 50–57) are inside the junction's middle segment A45–C54. The junction segment adds only its own closing nucleotides A45 (paired U25·A45 cWW, closing P2) and C54 (paired C54·G72 cWW, closing P3).
- **Interiors and closing residues:** the overlap covers the hairpin's closing pair *and* its interior; for the junction it is interior strand plus none of the junction's closing pairs. Consensus 53 (U49) is interior to both.
- **G46·C53 geometry:** FR3D annotates G46·C53 as **cWW, crossing 0** in 1Y26 X (`fr3d_1y26_X_basepair.json`, label 34–41), likewise G46·C53 cWW in 4FE5 B and 5E54 A, and G32·C39 cWW in 9IWF A. It is a canonical, nested Watson–Crick pair, isolated (no stacked cWW neighbour: the next nested cWW pairs are U25·A45 and C54·G72). The Milestone 3A note calling it a "non-canonical pair" was wrong in geometry terms; the pair is canonical cWW but isolated. Inside 46–53 FR3D reports no further nested cWW pairs; U49 and C50 make long-range cWS pairs with A76/U75 (crossing 1–2), part of the junction's tertiary core.

## 2. BGSU extraction methodology (documented vs observed)

Documented (Petrov et al. 2013, quoted): "Hairpin loops are delimited by two nucleotides (nt) that simultaneously make a canonical cWW base pair"; "The closing bases making canonical cWW pairs are considered to be part of the loop"; internal loops are "closed by two canonical cWW base pairs provided that on each strand … the nucleotides satisfy the flankSS relation or are adjacent", where flankSS holds between two nucleotides "if none of the nucleotides between them in the chain forms a canonical nested cWW pair"; "Three-way junctions (3WJ) are closed by three canonical cWW base pairs that bound up to three single-stranded regions"; "classification of junction motifs is the subject of a future publication and is not discussed here". Loops are extracted per type; a nucleotide's membership in several loop types is neither forbidden nor discussed; QA sets aside instances with missing coordinates or modified nucleotides (~9 % of loops).

Observed in the records:
- The hairpin follows the documented rule exactly: G46·C53 is a canonical cWW pair with no nested cWW pair between them, so FR3D extracts G46–C53 as a hairpin loop including the closing pair (signature starts with `cWW`).
- The junction loop's own base-pair signature contains **four** `cWW` entries although a 3WJ has three closing pairs: the fourth is the internal G46·C53 pair. The junction extraction therefore treats an isolated cWW pair inside a strand as part of the single-stranded region rather than as a helix that would split the junction into a 4WJ. This behaviour (helix = at least two stacked nested cWW pairs, isolated pairs tolerated inside loop strands) is consistent across 1Y26, 4FE5, 3RKF, 5E54 and 9IWF, but **it is not documented in the 2013 paper** and we did not locate junction-extraction documentation or code on rna.bgsu.edu (the `motifs/about` and `help` pages return 404). It is an interpretation from the records, flagged as such.
- Consequence: the Motif Atlas permits (and here systematically produces) a hairpin loop nested inside a junction strand. Both loops passed QA ("Valid loop") in the current release (4.15), and both are grouped into motif groups (HL_56046.1, 17 representative instances, annotated by BGSU as "Purine riboswitch"; J3_44163.4). The overlap is intrinsic to the source, not a mapping or aggregation artefact (0 duplicated units, raw CSV membership = `loops.json`).

## 3. Consistency across instances
- HL ∩ J3 at consensus 53 (62 chains, e.g. 1Y26 X, 4FE5 B, 3RKF A–D, 9IWF A/B): identical pattern, HL_56046.1 (8 nt, cWW-closed) nested in J3_44163.4 or a related J3 group; FR3D reports the equivalent isolated cWW pair where PDBe serves FR3D data.
- HL only (5E54 A/B, 5SWD A/B): the hairpin is present (G46·C53 cWW in 5E54 A) but the junction is recorded as J4 without the 50–57 strand, so no J3 covers 53.
- JL only (6P2H, 9LJN, 9LKC, 9LKE, 9LKF, 9LMC, 9V4U–9V51): in 9LKE A the equivalent segment G33…C40 is part of **IL_9LKE_001** (strands 9–12 / 32–39) and of two alternative J3 loops (J3_89880.1, J3_79739.1 with different helix delimitations), and no HL is extracted there; PDBe has no FR3D file for these 2025 entries, so the absence of the closing cWW pair could not be verified independently. Several of these chains carry two J3 loops covering 53 (alternative junction boundaries), which is why J3 has 93 loop observations for 76 instances; instance counts are deduplicated.

## 4. Conclusions
1. The overlap is real and expected: by the documented hairpin rule any isolated canonical cWW pair closes a hairpin, and by the (observed) junction rule an isolated cWW pair does not terminate a junction strand. HL and J3/J4 are therefore **potentially overlapping motif annotations**, not a mutually exclusive secondary-structure decomposition; a nucleotide can legitimately be in an HL and a JL within one experimental structure, and homologous consensus positions can carry different loop classes across structures (HL+J3, HL+J4-excluded, IL+J3).
2. The displayed counts (HL 66, JL 76 at consensus 53; HL∩JL 62; any 80 of 80 evaluable) are correct for distinct-instance counting per class and must not be combined or normalised.
3. The hairpin G46–C53 should not be described as an independently folded stem-loop; it is the cWW-closed J2/3 strand of the three-way junction, which the Motif Atlas itself labels "Purine riboswitch".
4. Remaining uncertainties: the exact junction-extraction rules for isolated cWW pairs are inferred from records, not documented; FR3D data for the 2025 entries are unavailable from PDBe, so their lack of a hairpin is explained only by the BGSU loop records (IL instead of HL). No error requiring a data change was found.
