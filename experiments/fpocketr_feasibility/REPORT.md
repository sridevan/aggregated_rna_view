## A. Scientific methodology

**What fpocketR is.** A Python wrapper (CLI `python -m fpocketR`) that cleans an input structure to its RNA chain(s), runs the unmodified `fpocket` binary with RNA-optimised parameters, and post-processes fpocket's output into pocket characteristics tables and figures (README; `pocket.py::run_fpocket` builds the command `fpocket -f <clean.pdb> -k <chain> -l <state> -m -M -i -D -A -p -w p`). It is "an RNA-specific wrapper for fpocket 4.0" (README); the algorithm (alpha-sphere detection, clustering, scoring) is fpocket's.

**RNA-specific optimisation (PNAS).** Four fpocket parameters were optimised on a curated set of RNA–drug-like-ligand complexes, searching 1,820 combinations in three rounds: minimum alpha-sphere radius `-m` 3.4 → **3.0 Å**, maximum radius `-M` 6.2 → **5.7 Å**, clustering distance `-D` 2.4 → **1.65 Å**, minimum alpha spheres per pocket `-i` 15 → **42**. The polarity filters `-A` and `-p` "were not effective at improving pocket detection" and were excluded from optimisation; fpocketR's defaults are `-A 3`, `-p 0.0` (README/usage table). The authors state that default fpocket "extensively over-predicts pockets in RNA" by treating grooves as pockets, and that fpocketR ranking "was deliberately made less dependent on scoring functions", relying on stricter geometric requirements that penalise highly solvent-exposed pockets (PNAS).

**Training and benchmark.** From 538 non-redundant HARIBOSS structures, 48 plausible drug-like complexes were selected (≤ 200 nt, ≤ 3.5 Å resolution, unique RNA, ligand QED ≥ 0.3, manual check of accessibility/depth); 38 were used, 13 training and 25 test (PNAS). A pocket is "known" when all three hold: pocket geometric centre within 4.5 Å of any ligand atom; ≥ 25 % of the pocket's alpha spheres within 3 Å of the ligand; ≥ 25 % of ligand atoms within 3 Å of the pocket (PNAS; implemented in `analyze.py::add_ligand_characteristics`, columns `Center_criteria`, `Pocket_overlap`, `Ligand_overlap`). Result: 100 % of known sites detected in training and test sets with a minimum positive predictive value of 78 %; the top-scoring pocket overlaps the known site 92 % of the time versus 63 % for default fpocket (PNAS). On the larger HARIBOSS library (365 complexes) fpocketR found 139 known and 237 novel pockets (Platform).

**Scores and features.** fpocketR reports, per pocket, fpocket's `Score`, `Druggability Score`, number of alpha spheres, pocket SASA, volume, mean local hydrophobic density, apolar alpha-sphere proportion, hydrophobicity and polarity scores (parsed from fpocket's `*_info.txt`), plus pocket shape descriptors NPR1/NPR2 and a shape class (rod/disc/sphere/balanced) computed from a PyMOL surface mesh of the alpha-sphere cores (`analyze.py::get_characteristics`, `add_basic_characteristics`), and, when a ligand is given, ligand QED and the three overlap criteria. Score use in the papers: "Only pockets with an fpocket score ≥ 0 were analyzed" (PNAS); a `--qualityfilter 0.4` was applied only to multistate NMR/cryo-EM ensembles "to allow comparison to previous studies" (PNAS, Platform). No general score threshold is recommended; the default `--qualityfilter` is 0.0 and pockets with Score > filter are marked `Pass`.

**Pocket nucleotides.** PNAS describes "coloring the nucleotides that contact alpha spheres from a pocket". In code, `PocketNT` is the set of residue numbers (author numbering) of the selected chain in fpocket's `pockets/pocketN_atm.pdb`, i.e. the atoms fpocket itself assigns as lining the pocket (`add_basic_characteristics`: `parsePDB(pocketN_atm.pdb).select('chain X').getResnums()` → `np.unique`). So membership is fpocket's own pocket-atom definition (atoms in contact with the pocket's alpha spheres), not every residue within a distance of the pocket, and no additional cut-off is introduced by fpocketR. **[inferred]** Pockets are reported independently; a nucleotide can appear in several pockets, and fpocketR does not merge or deduplicate overlapping pockets (it classifies each as Known/Novel and Pass/Fail).

**Ligand-bound vs ligand-free.** fpocketR always removes ligands before pocket detection (`clean_pdb` removes everything that is not polymer and all protein residues, keeping modified RNA residues), so holo structures are analysed as apo coordinates. Known sites were found in 9/10 holo and 9/10 apo structures of the test subsets, the holo miss being a low-QED ligand (phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate); for apo structures the paper notes that crystal packing often stabilised the apo state and that "pockets in apo structures do not always perfectly align with known ligand bind-sites" (PNAS). Ribosome: 7 of 17 RNA–ligand complexes recovered, with low-QED sites correctly not flagged in 9 of 10 cases (PNAS). Riboswitches (lysine, SAM-I, adenine, TPP) appear as examples but no class-level statistics are given (PNAS). The riboswitch of this project binds purines (QED of adenine/guanine/hypoxanthine is low to moderate; the method was tuned for "drug-like" ligands with QED ≥ 0.3), so recovery of the RF00167 site is plausible but not guaranteed by the published benchmarks **[inferred]**.

**Limitations stated by the authors.** Small training/test sets; best performance at ≤ 3.5 Å resolution (Platform); modelled structures from CASP recovered the known preQ1 pocket in only 25 % of models (Platform); long-range pockets are hard to analyse from secondary structure alone; default fpocket's groove pseudopockets motivate the stricter geometry (PNAS). Not discussed: artefacts at chain termini, incomplete nucleotides, modified nucleotides, physiological metabolite sites as a class. **Interpretation rule for this project:** a predicted pocket is a geometric statement about the deposited coordinates; it is not evidence of experimental ligand binding.

**Version sensitivity.** The README warns that "fpocket versions newer than v4.0.3 produce differences in predicted pocket outputs that have not been validated", recommends fpocket **v4.0.3 (x86_64)**, and the Bioconda recipe pins `fpocket =4.0.3`, `pymol-open-source ==2.5`, `python >=3.11,<3.12`, `prody ==2.3.0`, `rdkit ==2023.9.6`, `biopython ==1.83`. fpocket release notes after 4.0.3 include changes that plausibly alter results or parsing: 4.1.1 "adaptation on flexibility compute … will change results to prior versions", 4.1.3 insertion codes and split auth chain/residue numbering, 4.2.1 "compile M1/M2 chip molfile plugin", 4.2.2 mmCIF atom_site and chain-reading fixes. fpocketR's own defaults have been stable across releases (parameter defaults unchanged since 1.1.0; 1.3.0 added `--alignligand`, `--knownnt` and pocket-density figures).

### Option A: native arm64 build
- fpocket 4.0.3 has no arm64 binary; the README says fpocketR "may run on ARM-based macOS when compiled with fpocket v4.2.1 or later" (4.2.1 added the M1/M2 molfile plugin build). Compiling 4.0.3 from source on arm64 is not documented and would need the VMD molfile plugin fix that only landed in 4.2.1 **[inferred from release notes]**.
- Consequence: a native build means fpocket ≥ 4.2.1, which the authors explicitly flag as producing unvalidated output differences. Numerical agreement with the validated x86-64/4.0.3 pipeline cannot be assumed; a side-by-side comparison would be required before trusting it.
- Dependencies otherwise available natively (pymol-open-source 2.5 osx-arm64, prody/rdkit/biopython wheels for arm64 Python 3.11).
- Installation complexity: medium (micromamba or venv + compile); reproducibility: **poor** relative to the publication; performance: best.

### Option B: x86-64 macOS under Rosetta 2 (recommended)
- Rosetta 2 is already present, so no system change is needed. A self-contained x86-64 conda environment (micromamba binary for osx-64 placed in a project-local directory, `CONDA_SUBDIR=osx-64`) can install exactly the validated stack: `fpocket=4.0.3`, `pymol-open-source=2.5`, `python=3.11`, then `pip install fpocketr==1.3.6` (the README's "Conda + pip" route, or bioconda `fpocketr`).
- Everything runs as x86-64 under Rosetta, including Python, NumPy, RDKit and PyMOL (headless PyMOL is used for structure cleaning, surface meshes and figures; `-dpi 10` reduces ray-tracing cost). Known Rosetta caveats: no AVX-512; some conda packages compiled with AVX2 work; PyMOL's OpenGL rendering under Rosetta is a possible failure point (headless `pymol -cq` usually works) **[inferred, to verify in the pilot]**.
- Performance: fpocket on a 70–160 nt chain takes well under a second natively; Rosetta translation overhead (typically 20–50 %) is irrelevant at this scale; the PyMOL figure generation dominates runtime.
- Reproducibility: **high** (same binaries as the authors' recommendation); installation complexity: low-medium (one micromamba binary + one environment file, no sudo, no shell changes); risk: Rosetta-specific crashes in PyMOL or RDKit, mitigated by pinning the Bioconda versions.

### Option C: Linux x86-64 container or remote Linux
- Rancher Desktop is installed but stopped; starting it is a user action (it may prompt for VM configuration). A `linux/amd64` image with micromamba + bioconda `fpocketr` would be the most faithful reproduction of the published environment (Linux x86_64 is the first supported platform).
- On Apple Silicon, amd64 containers run under QEMU or Apple's Virtualization framework with Rosetta (Rancher offers VZ + Rosetta); QEMU emulation is slow (several-fold) and occasionally miscompiles SIMD paths; VZ+Rosetta is close to Option B performance but adds a VM layer and file-sharing of the coordinate cache.
- Remote Linux x86-64 (a lab server/HPC) removes all emulation concerns and is the right place for family-wide or ribosome-scale runs; it requires moving the cached mmCIF files and returning the output tables.
- Reproducibility: highest; complexity: medium (VM start, image build, bind mounts); risk: VM resource use and the dependency on the Rancher VM state.

### Recommendation for the Apple M3 Pro
Use **Option B** for the pilot and for RF00167: an isolated, project-local x86-64 micromamba environment under the already-present Rosetta 2, pinned to fpocket 4.0.3 / pymol-open-source 2.5 / Python 3.11 / fpocketR 1.3.6, created only after approval and never touching the project's pyenv environment, Homebrew or shell files. Keep **Option C (remote or containerised Linux x86-64)** as the reference environment for any larger-scale run and for an occasional cross-check that macOS-x86-64 and Linux-x86-64 produce identical pocket tables. Treat **Option A** as an experiment only, with explicit output comparison against Option B before any scientific use.

## C. Input and output specifications

**Input.** `--pdb` accepts a `.pdb` file, a `.cif` file or a PDB id (fetched; "Fetches .pdb format or .cif format if the .pdb is unavailable", `util.fetch_pdb`). Internally the structure is loaded into PyMOL, non-polymer molecules and protein residues are removed, and a cleaned **PDB** file `<name>_clean.pdb` is written and passed to fpocket (`clean_pdb`), so chain ids and residue numbers must survive a PyMOL round-trip into PDB format: single-character author chain ids and author residue numbers are preserved, insertion codes are preserved in PDB, but label (mmCIF) identifiers are not available downstream, and multi-character chain ids would be truncated **[inferred from PDB format; verify in pilot]**. fpocket is then restricted to the chosen chain with `-k <chain>` (author chain id, default = first RNA chain found by ProDy, `--chain` to override) and model `-l <state>`; `--state 0` loops over all models.

**Implications for our inputs.**
1. Our SASA pipeline isolates the chain by `label_asym_id` with gemmi. For fpocketR the equivalent is `--chain <auth_asym_id>` on a file containing that chain; passing a pre-isolated chain-only PDB file (written by gemmi from the updated mmCIF with author chain id and author numbering, altlocs resolved, hydrogens dropped, hetero RNA residues kept) is the cleanest way to guarantee that no other chain influences the alpha-sphere detection. fpocketR's own `clean_pdb` would additionally strip ligands/ions/water, so pre-cleaning is harmless and makes the input explicit. Removing ligands is what fpocketR does by design; the published validation was performed on ligand-removed (apo-like) coordinates, so a holo pocket is detected as a cavity left by the ligand, which is the intended behaviour, not an artefact, but it means a tightly packed bound ligand can leave a pocket that would partly collapse in a true apo structure (PNAS: apo pockets "do not always perfectly align").
2. Modified nucleotides: `clean_pdb` "preserves modified/heteroatom RNA residues" (docstring) by relabelling polymer HETATM records as ATOM; fpocket types atoms by element/name, so modified residues participate. ProDy's `nucleotide` selection used for chain detection may not recognise unusual residue names, so `--chain` should always be given explicitly **[inferred]**.
3. Incomplete nucleotides (missing phosphate at 5′ termini, missing bases) change the local surface and can create or remove alpha spheres; neither paper discusses this. We should record our SASA completeness status per nucleotide and flag pockets whose `PocketNT` include incomplete nucleotides **[proposal]**.
4. Scaffold/engineered nucleotides (9NBH/9NDD 160-mers, 6P2H flanks): pockets will be found on the scaffold too. Keep the complete deposited chain as input (so the aptamer core sits in its real context) but aggregate only nucleotides with a consensus match position; scaffold pockets stay in the per-instance data with `rfam_consensus_pos: null`.
5. Alternate locations: PyMOL keeps all altlocs on load and `cmd.save` writes them; fpocket reads PDB altlocs as separate atoms unless filtered **[inferred; fpocket's PDB reader behaviour to verify]**, which would double atoms and perturb alpha spheres. Pre-filtering to the highest-occupancy altloc (our SASA policy) avoids this. Multiple models: use model 1 (`--state 1`) for consistency with the mapping and SASA; `--state 0` is for ensembles.
6. Hydrogens: not required; fpocket works on heavy atoms and the authors' inputs were deposited structures without hydrogens **[inferred from standard fpocket usage]**. Our updated mmCIF files have none.
7. Termini: not discussed by the authors. The 5′/3′ ends of short aptamer constructs are often crystal-contact regions and frequently incomplete; terminal pockets should be flagged by position rather than removed **[proposal]**.

**Output (per run, directory `<out>/<name>_clean_out/`).** fpocket's native files: `<name>_clean_out.pdb` (input plus pocket alpha spheres as STP residues), `<name>_clean_info.txt` (per pocket: Score, Druggability Score, Number of Alpha Spheres, Total SASA, Polar/Apolar SASA, Volume, Mean local hydrophobic density, Mean alpha sphere radius, Mean alp. sph. solvent access, Apolar alpha sphere proportion, Hydrophobicity score, Volume score, Polarity score, Charge score, Proportion of polar atoms, Alpha sphere density, Cent. of mass – Alpha Sphere max dist, Flexibility), `<name>_clean_pockets.pqr` (all alpha spheres with radii) and `pockets/pocketN_atm.pdb` (pocket-lining atoms with their residue ids) / `pocketN_vert.pqr` (the pocket's alpha-sphere centres and radii). fpocketR adds `<name>_out_real_sphere.pdb` (alpha-sphere radii encoded in B-factors), `pockets/pocketN_surf.obj` meshes, 1D/2D/3D figures (`.png`, `.svg`, `.pse`) and a pocket characteristics CSV with the columns `Parameters, Name, PDB, State, Type, Filter, Pocket, Score, Drug_score, a-sphere, SASA, Volume, Hydrophobic_density, Apolar_a-sphere_proportion, Hydrophobicity_score, Polarity_score, PocketNT, Pocket_NPR1, Pocket_NPR2, Pocket_shape, Ligand_ID, Pocket_overlap, Ligand_overlap, Center_criteria, QED_score, Ligand_NPR1, Ligand_NPR2, Ligand_shape` (`analyze.py::get_characteristics`). Pocket numbering (`Pocket` = fpocket rank, 1 = best Score) is the identifier; pocket coordinates are the alpha-sphere centres in `pocketN_vert.pqr` and the centre of mass derivable from them.

**Extracting pocket nucleotides reproducibly.** Use `PocketNT` from the characteristics CSV (author residue numbers of `--chain`) as the primary definition, and cross-check it by parsing `pockets/pocketN_atm.pdb` ourselves (chain id, residue number, insertion code, residue name). Map `(pdb_id, auth_asym_id, author number, insertion code)` through the existing `InstanceIndex.resolve_unit` logic (as for BGSU unit ids) to `(pdb_id, label_asym_id, label_seq_id)` and the Rfam consensus position, keeping insert/flank residues with `rfam_consensus_pos: null`. Do not introduce a new contact distance: fpocket's lining-atom assignment is the published residue-level definition. Insertion codes: `PocketNT` collapses to integers (no insertion code), so for chains with insertion codes the `_atm.pdb` parse is authoritative **[inferred]**; RF00167 has none.

## D. Proposed aggregation methodology

Track quantity: **pocket occurrence frequency** per Rfam consensus position = (number of evaluable RNA chain instances whose nucleotide at that position lines at least one qualifying pocket) / (number of evaluable instances with an observed nucleotide at that position).

Rules
1. Per instance and position, the indicator is 1 if the nucleotide is in `PocketNT` of ≥ 1 qualifying pocket, 0 if the instance was evaluated and the nucleotide is observed (has coordinates) but in no qualifying pocket, and *missing* if the instance failed (fpocket error, unsupported chain) or the nucleotide has no coordinates / no consensus position. Several pockets containing the same nucleotide in one instance count once (set union per instance).
2. Qualifying pockets, initial proposal following the publications: all pockets with fpocket `Score ≥ 0` as in PNAS ("Only pockets with an fpocket score ≥ 0 were analyzed"), i.e. fpocketR's default `--qualityfilter 0.0` / `Filter = Pass`. No additional score, volume or rank threshold is applied in the primary track, because the authors give none for single structures; the 0.4 filter is documented only for ensembles. Store every pocket with its Score, Druggability, volume, SASA, alpha-sphere count, shape and `PocketNT` so that alternative filters (top-ranked only, Score ≥ 0.4, volume bands) can be evaluated later without rerunning fpocket.
3. Sensitivity analysis to report alongside the track (not in the UI): occurrence frequency computed with (a) all Pass pockets, (b) top-ranked pocket only, (c) Score ≥ 0.4. The gap between (a) and (b) measures how much low-ranked pockets inflate occurrence; if positions outside the known purine pocket reach high frequency only under (a), a reviewed filter becomes necessary.
4. Denominators follow the Milestone 3B convention: `n_observable_instances` (match residue with coordinates), `n_evaluated_instances` (fpocketR run succeeded for the chain), `n_observable_and_evaluated`, `n_positive`, `n_not_in_pocket`, `n_not_evaluated`. Frequency = positive / (observable ∧ evaluated), null when 0. Never divide by 84.
5. Construct differences: inputs are complete deposited chains, so scaffold regions produce pockets that only affect scaffold positions (consensus null) or the few consensus positions they carry; the denominators already restrict to observable instances. A secondary view restricted to instances covering the aptamer core (e.g. `fraction_clen_matched` within the family median band) can be computed from the per-instance data if construct effects are suspected; not needed for the first track.
6. Multi-copy entries (3RKF ×4, 9NBH/9NDD ×4, 5E54 ×2): each chain instance is evaluated separately on its own isolated chain; chains of one entry are not independent observations in the statistical sense, so report `n_entries` beside `n_instances` as the loop tracks do.
7. Retain per pocket: pocket id (`<seq_name>:pocket<N>`), rank, Score, Druggability, volume, SASA, alpha-sphere count, polarity/hydrophobicity, NPR1/NPR2/shape, centre coordinates, `PocketNT` with canonical ids and consensus positions, flags (contains incomplete nucleotides; touches a terminal nucleotide; lies on scaffold), fpocket/fpocketR versions and parameters.

## E. Validation strategy (RF00167)

Existing evidence: PDBe entity-level ligand contacts (3B `ligands.json`) with the pocket at consensus 25, 26, 51, 55, 56, 80, 81 (and 79, 52–54 less often); per-chain Arpeggio/RCSB contacts from the chain-specific investigation for 1Y26, 3RKF, 9NDD and 9NBH (`experiments/chain_specific_ligands/`), and the per-chain Arpeggio pilot for all 66 entries (`experiments/chain_specific_ligands/arpeggio_pilot/results/*.json`). Use the chain-level contacts (single-chain entities, or Arpeggio per chain) for per-structure validation; use the entity-level set only as a family-level reference.

Proposed comparisons
1. Known-site recovery per structure: run fpocketR with `--ligand <CCD>` on the holo file (ligand present in the input, removed by fpocketR, used only for the Known/Novel classification) so that fpocketR's own criteria (centre ≤ 4.5 Å, 25 % alpha-sphere overlap, 25 % ligand overlap) decide; record whether the top-ranked pocket is Known.
2. Residue overlap: Jaccard / recall between `PocketNT` (mapped to consensus) of the Known pocket and the chain-level contact residues; family-level: overlap between the occurrence track and the ligand-contact track at consensus 25–81.
3. Holo vs apo: 1Y26 (adenine), 4FE5 (hypoxanthine), 4LX5 (synthetic 29G) versus apo 5E54 A/B and 3IVN A/B (no default-track ligand; 9NBH has metals only and lacks aptamer coordinates). Compare whether the apo chains still show a pocket at the purine site and how its Score/volume differ.
4. Consistency across homologues: distribution of the Known pocket's rank, Score and volume across the 63 ligand-bound entries; positions with high occurrence outside the ligand site are candidate "novel" pockets or artefacts (crystal contacts, termini).
5. Published benchmark material: fpocketR's test suite (`fpocketR/tests/test_fpocketR.py`) and README examples (3E5C, 2L1V, 2GDI/8F4O TPP riboswitch, `--knownnt 19,20,42,43`) provide reference outputs for validating the installation; the PNAS training/test lists (SI) include an adenine riboswitch complex, which can be used as an external check that our run reproduces the published Known classification. Do not tune parameters on RF00167.

6. **Annotations UI:** one category "Predicted binding pockets" with one track "Pocket occurrence" using the existing frequency `ScaleDef` (fixed marks, shared axis, light→dark), concise tooltip ("Rfam residue 53 / In a predicted pocket in k of n RNA instances"), category ⓘ text noting ligand removal, fpocket 4.0.3 parameters and that predictions are geometric, not experimental evidence.

## G. Open questions for review

1. Approve creating the isolated x86-64 micromamba environment under Rosetta (Option B) in `tools/`; or prefer a Linux x86-64 host/container as the primary environment.
2. Input policy: pre-isolated single-chain PDB written by us (explicit, consistent with SASA) versus fpocketR's own cleaning of the full entry with `--chain`; the pilot should show they agree, but one must be declared canonical.
3. Qualifying pockets for the primary track: all `Score ≥ 0` pockets (publication default) versus top-ranked only; decide after the sensitivity analysis, not before.
4. How to present pockets that include incomplete nucleotides or terminal/scaffold nucleotides: flag only, or exclude from the primary occurrence count.
5. Whether RF00167's purine ligands (QED below or near the 0.3 drug-likeness bar used for training) make it an appropriate first validation family, or whether a second family with drug-like ligands (e.g. TPP or FMN riboswitch) should be run in parallel as a positive control.
6. Whether to run fpocketR with `--ligand` on holo inputs so that the Known/Novel classification is computed by the tool itself (ligand is still removed before detection), which gives a validation column for free.
7. Reproducibility record: pin fpocket 4.0.3 and fpocketR 1.3.6 permanently, and decide how to handle future fpocket versions (the authors flag unvalidated differences).
