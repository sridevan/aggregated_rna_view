## 1. Recognition modes in riboswitches (edges, stacking, metal ions, water, electrostatics)

### Takeaway
Riboswitches typically make many hydrogen bonds to the ligand from nucleobase edges, together with stacking on one or both faces. Purine-containing ligands are absorbed into the RNA as if they were a nucleotide, forming a base pair and a stack. Which edge forms the pairing varies a lot, even for the same ligand in different riboswitch classes. Phosphate- and carboxylate-bearing ligands (TPP, NAD+, GlcN6P, glycine, glutamine, SAM's methionyl tail) and anions such as F- are very often bound **through Mg2+ ions and their inner-sphere waters**, not through direct RNA contacts. Excluding ions and water from contact detection therefore hides the main recognition element for these moieties.

### Cited Findings
**General principles (review)**
- Huang & Lilley (2025, Q Rev Biophys) summarise ligand binding across all solved riboswitches: "The majority of riboswitches make multiple hydrogen bonds with their ligands. In many cases, the ligands can stack with nucleobases of the RNA. Inner-sphere metal ion interactions are found, along with cation–π interactions." In their conclusion, adenosine-containing ligands "form base pairs with the RNA, being both hydrogen bonded and stacked just like a section of the RNA itself. Electrostatic interactions can be important, and metal-ion-mediated contacts are frequently found." — [Huang & Lilley 2025, doi:10.1017/S0033583525100012](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012) (full text read)
- Edge diversity for the same ligand (from the same review): the SAM adenine forms a cis Hoogsteen:Watson–Crick pair with U in SAM-I (Montange & Batey 2006), a trans Watson–Crick:sugar-edge pair with G in SAM-III (Lu et al. 2008, PDB 3E5C), and a trans Hoogsteen:Watson–Crick pair with U in SAM-V (Huang & Lilley 2018, PDB 6FZ0). The NAD+ adenine forms a Watson–Crick:sugar-edge pair with G in NAD+-I and is stacked on one face only (PDB 6TF0); in NAD+-II it forms a trans Hoogsteen:Watson–Crick pair with A (Peng et al. 2023, PDB 8HB1). The cobalamin adenine forms a trans Watson–Crick:Hoogsteen pair with A in the AdoCbl riboswitch (Peselis & Serganov 2012). SAM conformations differ greatly between SAM-I (3GX5), SAM-III (3E5C) and SAM-V (6FZ0). — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- Serganov & Patel (2012, Annu Rev Biophys) describe how riboswitches work through the interplay of alternative ligand-free and ligand-bound conformations, and note that structures exist for "virtually all the major riboswitch classes in the ligand-bound state and, for several riboswitches, in the ligand-free state". — [Serganov & Patel 2012, doi:10.1146/annurev-biophys-101211-113224](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-101211-113224) (abstract)

**Purine riboswitch (RF00167)**
- The guanine riboswitch (B. subtilis xpt-pbuX) was solved bound to hypoxanthine at 1.95 Å (Batey et al. 2004). Huang & Lilley give PDB 4FE5 for this riboswitch; the original 2004 entry was 1U8D **[unverified]**, superseded by 4FE5 according to project memory. — [Batey et al. 2004, doi:10.1038/nature03037](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03037); [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- Serganov et al. (2004) solved the add A-riboswitch and xpt G-riboswitch. "The bound purines are held by hydrogen bonding interactions involving conserved nucleotides along their entire periphery. Recognition specificity is associated with Watson-Crick pairing of the encapsulated adenine and guanine ligands with uridine and cytosine, respectively." The PDB IDs are 1Y26 (adenine) and 1Y27 (guanine) **[unverified]**. — [Serganov et al. 2004, doi:10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018)
- Gilbert et al. (2006): Y74 forms a Watson–Crick pair with the purine ligand, and "the pyrimidine at position 74 is the sole determinant of purine riboswitch specificity". A C74U mutant with 2,6-diaminopurine bound gave ΔH = −40.3 kcal/mol, ΔS = −97.6 cal/mol/K and ΔG = −10.73 kcal/mol, so binding is enthalpy-driven. — [Gilbert et al. 2006, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2006.04.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2006.04.003)
- The other pocket residues come from background knowledge **[unverified]**: the purine is enclosed in the J1/2, J2/3 and J3/1 junction, with a base triple involving U22, U47 and U51 plus C/U74. The purine is fully encapsulated, so its WC, Hoogsteen and sugar edges are all contacted.

**TPP riboswitch (RF00059)**
- In E. coli thiM at 2.05 Å, "one subdomain forms an intercalation pocket for the 4-amino-5-hydroxymethyl-2-methylpyrimidine moiety of TPP, whereas another subdomain forms a wider pocket that uses bivalent metal ions and water molecules to make bridging contacts to the pyrophosphate moiety". The two pockets act as "a molecular measuring device", and "the central thiazole moiety is not recognized by the RNA", which explains how pyrithiamine pyrophosphate targets the riboswitch. The PDB ID is 2GDI **[unverified]**. — [Serganov et al. 2006, doi:10.1038/nature04740](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04740)
- In the Arabidopsis TPP riboswitch (2.9 Å), TPP is recognised by conserved residues in two distorted parallel "sensor" helices (Thore et al. 2006, PDB 2CKY **[unverified]**). Huang & Lilley report that the diphosphate interacts with one arm of the three-way junction "with direct contacts between the non-bridging oxygen atoms of the two phosphates and magnesium ions". — [Thore et al. 2006, doi:10.1126/science.1128451](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128451); [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- Edwards & Ferré-D'Amaré (2006) find that the thi-box prefers TPP over TMP by about 1000-fold. With monophosphorylated ligands, "the RNA elements that recognize the thiamine and phosphate moieties of the ligand move closer together", so the pocket adapts to the ligand. — [Edwards & Ferré-D'Amaré 2006, doi:10.1016/j.str.2006.07.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2006.07.008)

**SAM riboswitches (SAM-I RF00162 and others)**
- SAM-I from T. tengcongensis was solved at 2.9 Å (Montange & Batey 2006; Huang & Lilley list 3GX5 for SAM-I and 2GIS is the original entry **[unverified]**). As above, the SAM adenine forms a cis Hoogsteen:WC pair with U. — [Montange & Batey 2006, doi:10.1038/nature04819](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04819)
- SAM-II (Gilbert et al. 2008) is a pseudoknot. The ligand binds in a way different from SAM-I, and the structure includes the expression platform (Shine–Dalgarno). — [Gilbert et al. 2008, doi:10.1038/nsmb.1371](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.1371)
- SAM-III/S_MK box (2.2 Å): SAM is recognised at a three-way junction, and the Shine–Dalgarno sequence itself takes part in SAM recognition. The RNA "makes extensive interactions with the adenosine and sulfonium moieties of SAM but does not appear to recognize the tail of the methionine moiety". In the SAH complex the ligand adopts an alternative conformation and misses several key contacts. — [Lu et al. 2008, doi:10.1038/nsmb.1494](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.1494)
- SAM/SAH discrimination is electrostatic. In SAM-V (6FZ0) the sulfonium sits next to the U20:A48:U9 triple, with the C4 carbonyl oxygens of U20 and U9 pointing at the sulfur at 3.1 Å. Neutral SAH lacks this interaction, and no heat is detected on SAH addition. A similar mechanism has been proposed for SAM-II (Doshi et al. 2012). — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- In SAM-V, the methionyl carboxylate of SAM is bound through the inner-sphere waters of an Mg2+ that is itself directly bonded to a backbone phosphate non-bridging oxygen. This is an outer-sphere, metal-mediated contact. — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)

**Metal ion-mediated recognition (other classes)**
- glmS ribozyme (2.5 Å): "Recognition of GlcN6P is achieved through coordination of the phosphate moiety by two hydrated magnesium ions as well as specific nucleobase contacts to the GlcN6P sugar ring." The cofactor causes no conformational change compared with the apo enzyme. — [Cochrane et al. 2007, doi:10.1016/j.chembiol.2006.12.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2006.12.005)
- Glycine riboswitch (V. cholerae domain II): "Glycine recognition is facilitated by a pair of bound Mg(2+) cations and governed by specific interactions and shape complementarity". Bound and unbound structures were both solved; Huang & Lilley cite 3OWW. — [Huang, Serganov & Patel 2010, doi:10.1016/j.molcel.2010.11.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2010.11.026)
- Fluoride riboswitch (T. petrophila): F- is "anchored in place through direct coordination to three Mg(2+) ions, which in turn are octahedrally coordinated to water molecules and five inwardly pointing backbone phosphates". The ligand makes no direct RNA contact at all; every contact goes through Mg2+. The PDB ID is 4ENC **[unverified]**. — [Ren, Rajashankar & Patel 2012, doi:10.1038/nature11152](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11152)
- ZTP riboswitch (1.80 Å): ZMP is bound through its base, ribose and phosphate. "Unique to nucleobase recognition by RNAs, the Z base is inner-sphere coordinated to a magnesium cation bound by two backbone phosphates." — [Trausch et al. 2015, doi:10.1016/j.chembiol.2015.06.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2015.06.007)
- NAD+-I (ADP domain; 6TF0 is the NADH complex): the adenosine is anti and "forms multiple hydrogen bonds across the width of the sugar edge of the penultimate C:G base pair". Two divalent ions sit at the narrow neck of the bulge, and one bonds directly to the diphosphate. The review adds that both ions are extensively dehydrated, each having exchanged three inner-sphere waters, and that ion m1 bonds N7 of A10, which it calls a rare direct Mg2+–N bond. — [Huang, Wang & Lilley 2020, doi:10.1261/rna.074898.120](https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.074898.120); [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- Glutamine-II (PDB 6QN3): the amide forms 2 H-bonds to the C1:G40 pair and the carboxylate H-bonds N4 of C39. The other carboxylate oxygen bonds directly to a metal ion, replacing one of its inner-sphere waters, and two inner-sphere waters H-bond to G18. — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- The M-box riboswitch senses Mg2+ itself, which binds through direct Mg2+–O coordination with phosphates (Dann et al. 2007). — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)
- Caution on assigning metals: a PDB survey found that "Mg2+ assigned near imine nitrogens derive mostly from poor interpretations of electron density patterns and are most often misidentified Na+, K+, NH4+ ions, water molecules or spurious density peaks… apart from few documented exceptions, Mg2+ ions do not bind to N7 atoms". Mn2+, Zn2+ and Cd2+ can bind N7. — [Leonarski, D'Ascenzo & Auffinger 2017, doi:10.1093/nar/gkw1175](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkw1175)

**Cation–π and other electrostatic contacts**
- Guanidine-I/II/III (5T83, 5NOM, 5NWQ): in each class the guanidinium cation is stacked over the face of a G or C (cation–π) and H-bonded to G nucleobases (G90 in guanidine-I, G9 in guanidine-II, G7 and G17 in guanidine-III) and to phosphate oxygens. The pockets contain only H-bond acceptors, which, together with the cation–π contact, discriminates guanidine from urea. Guanidine-I: "Every functional group of the ligand is recognized through hydrogen bonding to guanine bases and phosphate oxygens… further stabilized through cation-π interactions". — [Reiss, Xiong & Strobel 2017, doi:10.1016/j.str.2016.11.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2016.11.020); [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)

**Stacking and intercalation; other classes**
- c-di-GMP-I (V. cholerae, 2.7 Å): the ligand binds at a three-helix junction "that involves base-pairing and extensive base-stacking", and the symmetric ligand is recognised asymmetrically by both bases and backbone. The PDB ID is 3IRW **[unverified]**. — [Smith et al. 2009, doi:10.1038/nsmb.1702](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.1702)
- FMN riboswitch (F. nucleatum; structures with FMN, riboflavin and roseoflavin): FMN sits asymmetrically in a six-stem junction and is bound through the isoalloxazine ring and the phosphate. Per the review literature, the phosphate is bound through Mg2+ **[unverified in this session; abstract truncated]**. Warner et al. use PDB 3F4G (riboflavin complex) and report pocket volume above 160 Å³ and buriedness above 0.75. — [Serganov, Huang & Patel 2009, doi:10.1038/nature07642](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07642); [Warner, Hajdin & Weeks 2018, doi:10.1038/nrd.2018.93](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrd.2018.93)
- Lysine riboswitch (T. maritima, 174 nt; bound 1.9 Å and free 3.1 Å): lysine binds at a five-helix junction and "is specifically recognized through shape-complementarity". PDB 3DIL appears in Huang & Lilley; 3D0U (bound) and 3D0X (apo) appear in Kligun 2013. — [Serganov, Huang & Patel 2008, doi:10.1038/nature07326](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07326); [Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682). A K+ ion mediating the lysine carboxylate contact (Garst et al. 2008) is from background knowledge **[unverified]**.
- preQ1-I (B. subtilis): a 34-nt pseudoknot is enough to recognise preQ1. Per background knowledge the ligand is WC-paired to a C and is stacked; PDB 3FU2 **[unverified]**. — [Klein, Edwards & Ferré-D'Amaré 2009, doi:10.1038/nsmb.1563](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.1563)
- NAD+-II (8HB1) binds two NMN molecules at two sites. Site 1 contacts G33 and C46. — [Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)

### Inferences
- For a per-part classifier, the ligand base is often a full base-pairing partner. Purine C74/U74 WC, SAM-I cis HW, SAM-III trans WS, and preQ1 and NAD+ pairs all show this. Recording which RNA edge pairs with the ligand (and ideally the Leontis–Westhof family) would be more informative than "base contact". The edge differs between classes binding the same ligand, so cross-family comparisons should be done on the edge, not on the ligand identity.
- Mg2+ bridges dominate recognition of phosphate, diphosphate and carboxylate moieties: the TPP pyrophosphate, glmS GlcN6P phosphate, NAD+ diphosphate, the glycine and glutamine carboxylates, the SAM-V methionyl tail and the ZTP Z-base. For fluoride, every contact is Mg2+-mediated. With ions and water excluded, these riboswitches will appear to contact only half of the ligand. The project should at minimum flag ligands with a metal within ~2.4 Å (inner-sphere) or ~4.5 Å (outer-sphere), and show "metal-bridged phosphate/ribose" as its own category rather than as absence. This is a design suggestion, not from a source.
- Cation–π stacking (guanidine) and electrostatic C=O to sulfonium contacts (SAM-V) do not fit an H-bond/stack dichotomy. If they are tracked, they need their own label or a "stacking (cationic ligand)" flag.

### Gaps
- No review I found gives counts per riboswitch class of how many ligand contacts are metal-mediated versus direct. Huang & Lilley only say metal contacts are "frequently found".
- Exact pocket residue numbers for TPP (e.g. G40/A43 intercalation, G78/G60 metal sites), SAM-I (U57, A45), glmS, FMN, lysine and preQ1 could not be verified from abstracts in this session. Get them from the primary PDB entries, for example with fingeRNAt or with our own pipeline.
- Primary structure papers for NAD+-II, ZTP from other groups (Ren 2015, Jones & Ferré-D'Amaré 2015), c-di-GMP-II and preQ1-II/III were not examined.

## 2. rRNA antibiotic sites: decoding site, Tet-1, spectinomycin, PTC and exit-tunnel drugs; roles of Mg2+, water and modifications

### Takeaway
Antibiotic sites in rRNA are made almost entirely of RNA. Binding mixes direct H-bonds to bases and backbone, stacking, and many water-mediated (sometimes ion-mediated) contacts. In the 1.6–2.2 Å cryo-EM structures, water-mediated H-bonds often equal the direct ones in number (kasugamycin: 10 direct plus 10 water-mediated). Tetracycline binds almost entirely to the h34 backbone through Mg2+, and its only base-specific contact is stacking on C1054.

### Cited Findings
**Decoding site (16S h44) and aminoglycosides**
- The NMR structure of paromomycin bound to an A-site oligonucleotide shows the drug "binds in the major groove of the model A-site RNA within a pocket created by an A-A base pair and a single bulged adenine". — [Fourmy et al. 1996, doi:10.1126/science.274.5291.1367](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.274.5291.1367)
- The 2.5 Å crystal structure of paromomycin bound to an E. coli A-site oligo shows 13 direct H-bonds to bases and backbone. "In the best-defined site, 8 water molecules mediate 12 other hydrogen bonds". "Ring I of paromomycin stacks over base G1491 and forms pseudo-Watson-Crick contacts with A1408." Ring II makes direct and water-mediated H-bonds to the U1495·U1406 pair. The bulged A1492/A1493 are held by H-bonds between their phosphate oxygens and rings I/II. — [Vicens & Westhof 2001, doi:10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3)
- The T. thermophilus 30S was solved with paromomycin, streptomycin and spectinomycin (PDB 1FJG, per Paternoga 2023). These structures produced a model in which universally conserved A1492/A1493 act in decoding. — [Carter et al. 2000, doi:10.1038/35030019](https://doi.org/10.1038/35030019); [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- When cognate tRNA binds, A1492, A1493 and G530 change conformation and interact with the minor groove of codon–anticodon pairs. "By partially inducing these structural changes, paromomycin facilitates binding of near-cognate tRNAs." — [Ogle et al. 2001, doi:10.1126/science.1060612](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1060612)
- At position 1408, A1408G is the classic aminoglycoside resistance mutation and carries a fitness cost. A structural basis is given by — [Kondo & Koganei 2019, doi:10.3390/molecules25010159](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25010159) (title/abstract level only)
- Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund cite earlier work showing that nonspecific electrostatic contacts between cationic ligands and the phosphate backbone "account for at least one-half of the total binding energy in certain aminoglycoside-RNA interactions". — [Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013, doi:10.4161/rna.24682](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682) (full text read; secondary citation)

**High-resolution solvent and ion survey (17 compounds, 6 classes, 1.6–2.2 Å cryo-EM, E. coli 70S)**
- "Water molecules are visualized within the antibiotic binding sites that are preordered, become ordered in the presence of the drug and that are physically displaced on drug binding." Drugs that share a scaffold have conserved binding modes, "including ordered water molecules" and second-shell waters. — [Paternoga et al. 2023, doi:10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y) (full text read)
- Counts in the same study: kasugamycin forms 20 H-bonds (10 direct, 10 water-mediated); avilamycin 18 (15 direct, 3 water); tiamulin 6 (4 direct, 2 water). For direct H-bonds, the paper says the ratio between backbone and nucleobase contacts "varies, ranging from 31% (four from 13) for avilamycin to 83% (10 from 12) for spectinomycin". The paper does not say which of the two is the numerator; spectinomycin's site at h34 (C1063/C1066) suggests nucleobase contacts. Streptomycin makes **no** nucleobase-specific contacts: 14 H-bonds to the backbone of h2 and h18, plus uS12 Lys44. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- Tetracycline (Tet-1 site, 16S head) "forms at least six direct hydrogen bonds to the backbone of nucleotides in h34, and… the only sequence specific interaction is a stacking interaction with C1054", which explains its broad spectrum. Mg1 is octahedrally coordinated by three h33/h34 RNA oxygens, two oxygens from tetracycline rings B and C, and one water. Mg1 is **present without the drug**, and tetracycline displaces two of its waters. Mg2 is coordinated by ring A plus four waters that H-bond U965 (h31), and it appears only when the drug is bound. In omadacycline and pentacycline, Mg2 instead binds the G966 phosphate directly. A pentacycline ring D/E extends the stack on C1054. Earlier tetracycline structures: 1HNW (3.4 Å, 30S), 1I97, 4V9A. In the new data, the secondary Tet-2 site has an inverted orientation. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y); [Brodersen et al. 2000, doi:10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00216-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0092-8674(00)00216-6); [Pioletti et al. 2001, doi:10.1093/emboj/20.8.1829](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/20.8.1829)
- Spectinomycin (h34): two waters are each coordinated by a base O2 (C1063, C1066) and that residue's 2′-O. In the tetracycline and streptomycin sites, every bridging water is coordinated by phosphate or ribose oxygens. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- Hygromycin B: a putative K+ is coordinated by drug rings I, II and IV, G1497 and a water. The Mg2+ modelled in the 80S hygromycin structure is not compatible with this conformation. Streptomycin ring III waters belong to an Mg2+ octahedron. Kasugamycin O6 binds a water that coordinates Mg2+. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- Lincomycin (PTC): five base nitrogens (A2059, G2061, A2062, A2503, C2611) coordinate four bridging waters. The waters bound to A2058/A2059 stay stable at physiological temperature in MD. A neural network trained on H-bonds showed that water-mediated H-bonds affect the antibiotic's conformation. The paper also notes that a single water-mediated contact between erythromycin and the ribosome had earlier been found critical for binding. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)

**23S PTC and exit tunnel**
- D. radiodurans 50S with chloramphenicol, clindamycin, erythromycin, clarithromycin and roxithromycin: "antibiotic binding sites are composed exclusively of segments of 23S ribosomal RNA at the peptidyl transferase cavity and do not involve any interaction of the drugs with ribosomal proteins… Our results also show the importance of putative Mg+2 ions for the binding of some drugs." — [Schlünzen et al. 2001, doi:10.1038/35101544](https://doi.org/10.1038/35101544)
- E. coli 70S with erythromycin, telithromycin, clindamycin and chloramphenicol at about 3.3–3.4 Å: binding modes differ from the archaeal and Deinococcus structures, and the identity of 23S nucleotides 752, 2609 and 2055 explains part of the species spectrum. — [Dunkle et al. 2010, doi:10.1073/pnas.1007988107](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1007988107)
- Linezolid binds the 50S A-site cleft near the catalytic centre (H. marismortui), competing with A-site substrates. Per background knowledge it contacts A2451, C2452 and U2504 and stacks on them; PDB 3CPW **[unverified]**. Warner et al. describe the linezolid pocket as large and highly buried. — [Ippolito et al. 2008, doi:10.1021/jm800379d](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm800379d); [Warner et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrd.2018.93)
- Macrolide selectivity depends on 23S position 2058 (A in bacteria, G in eukaryotes). A single rRNA mutation made yeast ribosomes bind telithromycin in the tunnel. — [Svetlov et al. 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-23068-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23068-1). That A2058 dimethylation by Erm is the main macrolide resistance mechanism is background knowledge **[unverified here]**.

**rRNA modifications**
- Cfr C8-methylation of A2503 (m8A2503) is "one of the most abundant and clinically prevalent resistance mechanisms to PTC-acting drugs in Gram-positive bacteria". Structures show that it triggers an allosteric rearrangement of A2062, which lowers the potency of some PTC inhibitors (iboxamycin and tylosin get around it in different ways). — [Aleksandrova et al. 2024, doi:10.1038/s41589-023-01525-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01525-w)
- The 2.3–2.5 Å T. thermophilus ribosome structures allowed rRNA modifications to be modelled, showing their contacts with mRNA, tRNA and the rRNA interior. — [Polikanov et al. 2015, doi:10.1038/nsmb.2992](https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.2992)

### Inferences
- Tetracycline, streptomycin and aminoglycoside phosphate contacts on h44 are backbone- and metal-dominated. A per-part classifier with ions removed will show tetracycline as "phosphate/ribose plus one stack (C1054)" and will miss the Mg1/Mg2 bridges entirely. Mg1 exists in apo ribosomes as well, so an apo-versus-holo comparison of that pocket must keep the ion to stay meaningful.
- Nearly half the H-bonds in high-resolution antibiotic sites can be water-mediated (kasugamycin 50%, paromomycin about 48% = 12/25). Ignoring waters will understate the involvement of base edges and ribose. It is also resolution-dependent: older 3–3.5 Å structures will show no waters even when bridges exist, so "no water-mediated contact" must be treated as "not observable" rather than "absent". This matches the project's rule on missing versus absent.
- Modified nucleotides (m8A2503, m6,6A2058 via Erm, and the m2A2503/Ψ in the PTC) sit at drug sites. Contacts to them should be mapped through the nonstandard-nucleotide handling the project already has, not dropped.

### Gaps
- Exact residue lists for Tet-1 (e.g. G1053, C1054, U1196, h31 U965/G966) beyond those quoted above, and spectinomycin's G1064/C1192 contacts, were not checked against primary coordinates in this session.
- I found no source that quantifies, across all PDB antibiotic–rRNA complexes, the fraction of contacts that are Mg2+-mediated.
- The specific 2023 PDB IDs (8CF1, 8CGJ, 8CGU, ... from Paternoga) appeared only in search snippets and were not mapped to individual drugs.

## 3. Conformational change: apo vs holo, induced fit vs conformational selection; implications for comparing pockets

### Takeaway
Riboswitch ligand-free states range from largely preorganised to largely unfolded. Many binding pockets close or form only when the ligand binds, through junction zippering or pseudoknot capture. Binding often looks like conformational selection or capture of transiently sampled states followed by local induced fit. Unusual nucleotide conformations (syn, C2′-endo, unpaired) are enriched in ligand pockets and may already exist before binding. Pocket comparisons across apo and holo structures therefore need care: an "absent contact" in an apo chain is usually an absent pocket, not a different recognition mode.

### Cited Findings
- In the adenine riboswitch observed by mix-and-inject XFEL crystallography, four structures were captured: two apo conformers, a ligand-bound intermediate at 10 s, and the final bound state. "The three-way junction and the P1 switch helix of the two apo conformers are notably different from those in the ligand-bound conformation"; the intermediate shows changes in the pocket that accommodate the ligand. — [Stagno et al. 2017, doi:10.1038/nature20599](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20599)
- The V. vulnificus add riboswitch has three stable states, with a temperature- and Mg2+-dependent pre-equilibrium between two ligand-free conformations. This shows that the two-state model is inadequate. — [Reining et al. 2013, doi:10.1038/nature12378](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12378)
- In SAM-II, the unliganded pseudoknot is "highly transient", and SAM restrains it "through a conformational capture mechanism". — [Haller et al. 2011, doi:10.1038/nchembio.562](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.562)
- The free SAM-I aptamer samples "an ensemble of states that minimizes the energy barrier between the free and bound states". — [Stoddard et al. 2010, doi:10.1016/j.str.2010.04.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2010.04.006)
- In the purine riboswitch "the internal bubble zippers up to form the purine binding pocket". — [Serganov et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018)
- The thi-box pocket adapts to ligand length: TMP-like ligands pull the pyrimidine- and phosphate-sensing elements closer together. — [Edwards & Ferré-D'Amaré 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2006.07.008)
- Counterexample: GlcN6P "does not induce a conformational change" in glmS, where the ligand is a catalytic cofactor. — [Cochrane et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2006.12.005)
- The lysine riboswitch was solved bound at 1.9 Å and free at 3.1 Å. Pocket nucleotides A8, A9 and A77 are syn in both the free (3D0X) and bound (3D0U) forms, so the pocket is preorganised. The preQ1 riboswitch is unstructured when free and shows large differences. — [Serganov et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07326); [Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682)
- "Conformational readout": in 33 representative pockets from 10 RNA groups, ligand pockets (especially in "adaptive" targets such as aptamers and riboswitches) are enriched in syn, C2′-endo/C4′-exo and non-paired nucleotides, and depleted in cWW pairs, compared with all computed RNA pockets and with RNA–protein interfaces. These rare-conformation nucleotides are preferentially in direct contact with the ligand. Syn nucleotides are enriched in H-bond, vdW and hydrophobic contacts. An earlier ribosome study found the same "RNA signature" (unpaired, syn, unusual pucker) at antibiotic sites. — [Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682)
- On the ribosome, the A1492/A1493/G530 flip is induced by cognate tRNA and partly mimicked by paromomycin. Tetracycline's Mg1 and some waters are preordered in the apo site, while other waters become ordered or are displaced on binding. — [Ogle et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1060612); [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- In the RRE, MD hydration analysis found that one recognition conformation keeps an ordered hydration scaffold without its partner (preorganised), whereas another reorganises its waters. This is a peptide system, used here only as an analogy for the solvent role in preorganisation. — [Ghosh et al. 2026, doi:10.1016/j.crstbi.2026.100184](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crstbi.2026.100184)

### Inferences
- For per-position aggregation across chains in one Rfam family: contact conservation should use as its denominator the chains that bound a ligand (holo). Apo chains form a separate "pocket state" stratum. For RF00167 the apo structures (and the Stagno 2017 apo conformers) would otherwise dilute conservation. This fits the project rule against dividing by the total instance count.
- A pocket defined geometrically (fpocketR) on an apo chain may not exist, or may be mis-shaped, in junction-zippering riboswitches. Comparing pockets across apo and holo should be reported as a state change, not as variable recognition.
- Syn and C2′-endo flags at contact positions could be a useful secondary annotation if the pipeline already computes torsions.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic, quantitative survey of apo versus holo RMSD or pocket volume across all riboswitch classes in this session.
- How the induced-fit and conformational-selection balance differs between classes (beyond SAM-II capture, adenine three-state and lysine preorganisation) was not quantified.

## 4. Statistical surveys of interaction types in RNA–ligand complexes

### Takeaway
Across PDB surveys, H-bonds and stacking dominate RNA–small-molecule recognition, unlike the hydrophobic-driven recognition in proteins. Nucleobase contacts outnumber backbone contacts by roughly 2:1, and Padroni et al. reported an "unexpected paucity of backbone interactions, even for cationic ligands". Water- and ion-mediated contacts look rare in bulk statistics because most structures do not resolve them. In the highest-resolution subset, water bridges become the most frequent interaction type.

### Cited Findings
- Padroni et al. (2020): from a PDB survey, "RNA recognition is driven mostly by stacking and hydrogen bonding interactions, while protein recognition is instead driven by hydrophobic effects". They report "stacking and cation-π interactions that favor purine and guanine recognition" and "an unexpected paucity of backbone interactions, even for cationic ligands". — [Padroni, Patwardhan, Schapira & Hargrove 2020, doi:10.1039/d0md00167h](https://doi.org/10.1039/d0md00167h) (abstract). A search-engine summary (not verified in full text) said the dataset was small, with 37 unique ligands on 14 targets and 770 π-stacking contacts mostly with A and G; treat these numbers as **unverified**.
- Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund (2013), 10 RNA groups from the PDB: "nucleobase-specific interactions… and non-specific interactions (… RNA backbone) make up 65% and 35% of RNA-ligand hydrogen bonds, respectively". Pockets are depleted in cWW pairs and enriched in unpaired, non-canonical, syn and C2′-endo nucleotides. They also cite earlier work: "interactions between the ligands and the Watson-Crick edge of the RNA were frequently observed", and "pseudo base pairs using the Watson-Crick edge in combination with stacking interactions were frequently involved in specific ligand recognitions" (Kondo et al.). Water was excluded from their interface calculation. — [Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013, doi:10.4161/rna.24682](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682) (full text read)
- fingeRNAt (2022) analysed 207 RNA–ligand structures (180 X-ray, 27 NMR) across 9 interaction types: H-bonds 5026, lipophilic 3582, cation–anion 899, water-mediated 151, π-stacking 146, halogen 6, and fewer ion-mediated contacts. 68% of structures contain any water, but only 22% of those (15% overall) have a water-mediated RNA–ligand contact. In the 8 complexes at ≤1.5 Å, water-mediated contacts (74) outnumber H-bonds (62) and lipophilic contacts (42). A second H-bond length peak at 3.7 Å may reflect bridges through unmodelled waters. For comparison, over 85% of protein–ligand crystal structures (n = 392) have at least one bridging water. Ion-mediated contacts are split into Mg2+, K+, Na+ and other. — [Szulc et al. 2022, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783) (full text read). The π-stacking count depends on the algorithm; the authors note that PLIP-based counts differ.
- Paternoga et al. (2023) give ribosome-specific numbers: water-mediated H-bonds range from 0% to 50% of all drug–ribosome H-bonds depending on the drug (kasugamycin 10/20; avilamycin 3/18; tiamulin 2/6). See section 2. — [Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)
- Vicens & Westhof (2001): for paromomycin at the A site, 13 direct and 12 water-mediated H-bonds through 8 waters. — [Vicens & Westhof 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3)
- Warner et al. (2018): before about 2012, RNA ligands were mostly "highly basic… and planar molecules capable of intercalation between and stacking on RNA bases". High-quality RNA pockets (FMN riboswitch, linezolid site) are large (>160 Å³) and buried (>0.75), like protein drug pockets. — [Warner, Hajdin & Weeks 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrd.2018.93) (full text read)
- Resources: HARIBOSS is a curated database of RNA–small molecule structures from X-ray, NMR and cryo-EM (Panei et al. 2022), and HARIBOSS++ (2026 preprint) adds per-family analysis and 3D characterisation. — [Panei et al. 2022, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btac483](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac483); [Marengo et al. 2026, doi:10.64898/2026.09.07.749797](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.07.749797). R-BIND 2.0 covers bioactive RNA-targeting ligands and their secondary-structure motifs. — [Donlic et al. 2022, doi:10.1021/acschembio.2c00224](https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.2c00224)

### Inferences
- With waters excluded, base edges (WC especially) and stacking should dominate the project's contact classes. This is consistent with the surveys and is not an artefact. Backbone contacts at roughly a third of H-bonds is a plausible benchmark to check against.
- The fingeRNAt result that water bridges are the most frequent contact type at ≤1.5 Å, but rare in bulk, shows that a "water/ion-mediated" class would be heavily resolution-confounded. If it is added, it should come with a resolution stratum or a per-structure "solvent modelled: yes/no" eligibility flag as the denominator.
- fingeRNAt's SMARTS-based, ion-specific (Mg/K/Na/other) and water-mediated detectors, with documented geometric criteria (its S20 Table), could be reused as a reference implementation for adding metal- and water-mediated contact categories.

### Gaps
- I could not access the Padroni 2020 full text, so its per-atom H-bond tallies (N7, O6, 2′-OH, phosphate), its edge breakdown and its exact dataset size are unverified.
- I found no survey reporting Leontis–Westhof edge usage (WC vs Hoogsteen vs sugar) for ligand contacts with explicit fractions. Kligun 2013 (citing Kondo) only says WC-edge contacts are "frequent".
- I found no PDB-wide fraction of RNA–ligand contacts that are Mg2+-mediated. fingeRNAt's ion-mediated counts were below the shown tallies but were not given separately in the text I extracted.
