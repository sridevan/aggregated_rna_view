# Shared rRNA coordinates make this study publishable

The study is novel and worth doing, but not for the reason that first comes to mind. **An archive-wide inventory of ribosome–drug sites already exists several times over.** HARIBOSS++, SPIRAL, FURNA and the DrugPred_RNA set each hold hundreds of ribosomal pockets, which is the same scale as our 568 entries. Interaction-fingerprint clustering by drug class was published by SPIRAL in 2026. Cross-species variation of drug-binding residues was published by the Melnikov lab in two 2025 Cell Reports papers. What no resource or paper does is put **every PDB copy's contacts on one model-based rRNA coordinate system**, compare bacterial, eukaryotic cytosolic, parasite and mitochondrial ribosomes **from experimental structures** on that system, report per-position occupancy with explicit denominators, and attach curated 3D motifs (FR3D pairs and triples, Motif Atlas loops) to the contacted positions. That combination is the genuine gap. The largest technical risk is Rfam itself: bacterial and eukaryotic rRNA are separate families, so the "shared" axis needs a validated cross-family bridge, and mitochondrial rRNA may map only partly. The largest scientific risk is that much selectivity is carried by ribosomal proteins, ion and water networks, or site choice rather than rRNA base identity, which an rRNA-position view captures only partly. The recommendation is to proceed, framed as a quantitative, quality-aware, cross-kingdom atlas with one or two non-obvious biological results. Before claiming "first", inspect RiboVision2's data layers and BGSU RNA 3D Hub, and prove the cross-family bridge on known determinants.

Statements marked *(project data)* come from our own inventory and analyses. Statements marked *(inference)* are this report's reasoning. Everything else is cited.

## Four databases already hold the inventory, so the inventory is not the contribution

Ribosomes dominate every structure-based RNA–ligand collection because they dominate the PDB. In **HARIBOSS++, rRNA accounts for 60.9% of pockets**, which the authors attribute to "experimental bias toward ribosomal structures in the PDB". HARIBOSS++ defines a pocket as residues within 6 Å, adds DSSR secondary structure, SiteMap descriptors and BGSU equivalence classes for apo–holo pairing, and extends pockets to sites shared with proteins. Chains are assigned to 12 coarse families by keyword, with Rfam used only as a chain-level cross-check ([HARIBOSS++ preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.07.749797v1.full)). **SPIRAL** curates 1,098 structures and 1,137 binding events, of which **543 (47.8%) are rRNA**. Its DSSR fingerprints separate ribosomal sub-sites: cluster R-C1 (n = 208) is 98% aminoglycosides at the decoding A-site, and a PTC/exit-tunnel cluster holds the macrolides and lincosamides ([SPIRAL, PMC13228370](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13228370)). **FURNA** annotates ligand-binding nucleotides for every PDB RNA chain of 10 nt or more and assigns Rfam families, but keeps binding sites in each chain's own numbering ([FURNA, PMC11309384](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11309384)). **DrugPred_RNA** built a ribosome set of 497 PDB entries, 613 pockets and 217 ligands, and predicted 31% of ribosomal pockets druggable ([Rekand & Brenk 2021, PMC8389535](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8389535)).

The ribosome-specific portals solve different problems. **RiboXYZ** standardises polymer names (Ban nomenclature, one HMM per class) but has no shared rRNA residue numbering, and it transfers 5 Å ligand sites pairwise between two chains with a global sequence alignment ([Kushner et al. 2023](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/D1/D509/6777803)); its recent repository activity concerns exit-tunnel geometry, not ligands ([riboxyz GitHub](https://github.com/rtviii/riboxyz)). **RiboVision2** maps any wwPDB ribosome to preloaded 16S/18S and 23S/28S alignments of 152 species by MAFFT and displays phylogenetic and structural properties in 1D, 2D and 3D ([RiboVision2 data page](https://apollo2.chemistry.gatech.edu/AboutRiboVision2/ribovision_data); [McCann et al. 2024](https://sites.gatech.edu/icool/2024/10/17/a-new-bioinformatics-tool-ribovision2)). Whether it has a ligand-contact layer could not be confirmed. Generic RNA–ligand resources tend to discard ribosomes instead: DRLiPS kept only 51 of 513 ribosomal complexes, the 16S A-site ([DRLiPS workflow](https://web.iitm.ac.in/bioinfo2/DRLiPS/Details.html)), and R-BIND excludes ribosomal ligands by design ([R-BIND 2.0](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.03.14.484334.full.pdf)). PDBe-KB aggregated views are UniProt-centric, with no RNA or Rfam-based aggregation found ([ELIXIR TeSS](https://dev.tess.elixir-europe.org/events/pdbe-kb-aggregated-views-presenting-pdb-data-in-a-different-context-131d7dc8-cb41-4b94-b565-eb902a25b347)).

| Resource | What it does | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| HARIBOSS / HARIBOSS++ (2022, 2026 preprint) | Curated RNA–ligand pocketome, 6 Å pockets, 60.9% rRNA, DSSR, SiteMap, mixed RNA/protein pockets, BGSU classes for apo–holo pairs | No shared rRNA numbering, no Rfam positions, no cross-kingdom comparison, no Motif Atlas loops |
| SPIRAL (2026 preprint) | 543 rRNA binding events, DSSR fingerprints, clustering by drug class, coarse motif context (hairpin, junction, pseudoknot) | Structure-local only; no common coordinates; motif context is DSSR categories, not curated 3D motif families |
| FURNA (2024) | Ligand-binding nucleotides for all PDB RNA chains, Rfam family labels | Binding sites in chain numbering; Rfam is a label, not an axis |
| DrugPred_RNA set (2021) | 497 ribosome entries, 613 pockets, druggability per pocket | No cross-species mapping, no per-copy statistics |
| RiboXYZ (2023) | Nomenclature, pairwise 5 Å site transfer | No shared numbering, no aggregation across copies |
| RiboVision2 (2024) | Cross-species alignments, any wwPDB structure, 1D/2D/3D views | Ligand layer unconfirmed; alignment by MAFFT, not structure |
| Melnikov lab, Cell Rep 2025 (two papers) | Drug-binding residue evolution across bacteria and eukaryotes from SILVA | Residues from ~29 representative structures; sequence-based; no mitochondria, no archaea |

The consequence is direct *(inference)*: any manuscript or grant that leads with "the first systematic collection of ribosome–drug structures" will be rejected by a reviewer who knows SPIRAL or HARIBOSS++. The inventory is infrastructure; the coordinate system and what it reveals are the contribution.

## The real gap is a structural, cross-kingdom comparison on one axis

The closest structural survey of ribosomal antibiotic sites is sixteen years old and bacterial. David-Eden, Mankin and Mandel-Gutfreund analysed **65 antibiotic sites** (rRNA atoms within 6 Å) from *T. thermophilus*, *E. coli*, *H. marismortui* and *D. radiodurans* and found an RNA signature: unpaired bases, *syn* conformations, unusual sugar puckers, high conservation and high network centrality. **No eukaryotic or mitochondrial ribosome entered as a binding-site source** ([NAR 2010](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/38/18/5982/1065889)). Every structure that would make the analysis cross-kingdom appeared later: the yeast 80S panel of 16 inhibitor structures ([Garreau de Loubresse et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13737)), aminoglycosides on yeast 80S with **multiple sites in both subunits** ([Prokhorova et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1715501114)), *Plasmodium* with emetine ([Wong et al. 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/03080)) and mefloquine ([Wong et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmicrobiol.2017.31)), *Leishmania* with paromomycin ([Shalev-Benami et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01664-4)), and the human mitoribosome with streptomycin ([STIAS summary](https://www.stias.ac.za/publications/structural-basis-of-streptomycin-off-target-binding-to-human-mitoribosome/)), tigecycline ([Li et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49797-7)) and linezolid ([Bibel et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39907106/)). A 2026 cross-kingdom repeat is newly possible and has not been done, on the evidence found.

The Melnikov papers are the prior art that matters most and must be engaged head-on. The eukaryotic study defined **58 drug-binding residues (26 in 18S)** at 4.3 Å from 29 representative structures, then scanned 8,563 eukaryotes in SILVA. About 23% of eukaryotes carry 1–4 fixed changes relative to human, and some lineages differ more from human than human does from bacteria. Organelle-encoded rRNA was excluded and archaea were not analysed ([Chan et al. preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.28.625670v1.full)). The bacterial study found that many rRNA features assumed universal to bacteria occur only in subsets, and it used **AlphaFold3 predictions** rather than experimental structures for divergent sites ([Ekemezie et al. 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00649-7)). Our study is complementary, not duplicative *(inference)*: they ask how the sequence of a fixed residue set varies across the tree; we can ask which positions are actually contacted, by how many copies, with what interaction and motif context, across every experimental structure in every kingdom, including mitoribosomes. Their 58-residue set with *E. coli*, yeast and human correspondence (Table S2) is also a ready-made benchmark for our cross-family mapping.

The scattered state of selectivity knowledge is the strongest argument for a shared axis. Well-established determinants are few: **16S A1408** (A in bacteria and mitochondria, G in the eukaryotic cytosol; A1408G confers resistance to 6′-amino aminoglycosides) ([Matt et al. 2012, PMC3390888](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390888)), **23S A2058** for macrolides ([Zhang et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2005.09.103)), and the **12S A1555G and C1494T** deafness alleles, which raise aminoglycoside susceptibility of mitochondrial hybrid ribosomes 4- to 16-fold ([PMC3390888](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390888); [PMC2897308](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2897308)). They are reported in at least four numbering systems, and single-position rules already break: *Echinococcus multilocularis* and *Acanthamoeba castellanii* carry **adenine at mitochondrial 2058** and are macrolide-susceptible ([PMC1196245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1196245/)). No source tabulated binding-site identity per drug class per kingdom. For oxazolidinones, phenicols, tetracyclines, the emetine/pactamycin site and the GTPase-centre site, no list of kingdom-discriminating positions was found at all. The reviews' refrain that ribosomes are "too conserved to target selectively" ([J Microbiol review](https://www.jmicrobiol.or.kr/journal/view.php?number=3061)) has not been **quantified** structure-wide *(inference)*. Counting, per site and drug, how many contacted consensus positions differ between kingdoms is a well-posed question this study answers.

Tigecycline shows why the comparison changes conclusions. In one paper it binds the human mitoribosome at therapeutic concentration but **"does not bind to human 80S ribosomes under physiological concentrations"**; at high concentration human and yeast 80S bind it at a different, conserved site that restricts L1-stalk movement ([Li et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49797-7)). Telithromycin alone among tested inhibitors acts differently on mitoribosomes and bacteria ([eLife reviewed preprint 103699](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/103699v2)). Selectivity is often about **which of several sites is occupied**, which favours a survey of all contact sets per structure, secondary sites included, over a determinant lookup table *(inference)*.

The motif layer is also unclaimed. Ligand "motif" vocabularies are secondary-structural (Inforna, R-BIND 2.0, RNALigands, fpocketR) or base-pair-graph based (RNAmigos), and no resource found annotates ribosomal drug sites with Motif Atlas loop classes or recurrent 3D modules ([Petrov et al. 2013, PMC3854523](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3854523/); [RNAmigos2, PMC11928640](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11928640/)). SPIRAL's motif context is coarse DSSR categories ([PMC13228370](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13228370)). On its own, though, attaching loop IDs to the h44 A-site, the PTC junction or the GTPase-centre loops is annotation, not discovery *(inference)*. The motif layer earns its place if it finds selectivity that coincides with a change in a non-canonical pair, triple or loop geometry rather than base identity.

## Rfam's per-kingdom families are the hardest technical problem

Rfam is a strong choice inside a family and a weak one across families. Its CM consensus positions are reproducible and versioned, and they come with published PDB mappings: RF02541 alone lists 1,730 structures ([Rfam RF02541](https://rfam.org/family/RF02541)), and BGSU publishes per-family column-to-unit maps ([BGSU Rfam alignments](https://rna.bgsu.edu/data/alignments/rfam/)). Rfam 15 revised 65 families using 3D structures ([Rfam 15, PMC11701678](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11701678/)), though whether the rRNA families were among them is unconfirmed. But bacterial SSU (RF00177), eukaryotic SSU (RF01960), bacterial LSU (RF02541) and eukaryotic LSU (RF02543) are **separate CMs**, with archaeal and microsporidial families beside them ([Rfam motif RM00003](https://rfam.org/motif/RM00003)). The LSU families share clan CL00112, but a clan is a grouping, not a position map. A "cross-kingdom consensus position" therefore does not exist until we build a family-to-family correspondence *(inference)*.

There are three practical bridges, and the study should use one as primary and another as a check *(inference)*. The first is structural: superpose representative bacterial and eukaryotic ribosomes and pair consensus positions whose nucleotides superpose in the conserved core, which the Petrov–Williams work shows is well defined because eukaryotic expansions insert into the common core without perturbing local conformation ([Petrov et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1407205111)). The second is a curated cross-domain alignment such as RiboVision's, at the cost of importing MAFFT decisions. The third is a CM-to-CM alignment of consensus columns. Whichever is chosen, the bridge must be validated on the known determinants (1408, 1491, 1555/1494 equivalents, 2058, the PTC nucleotides 2062/2504/2506/2585/2602) and on the Melnikov 58-residue correspondence table before any cross-kingdom statistic is reported. The project rule that consensus positions come only from validated mappings applies doubly here: unbridged positions must be reported as unbridged, not forced.

Mitochondria are where the bridge is most likely to fail. Mitochondrial rRNAs are reduced and divergent, the accessions and PDB coverage of any mitochondrial Rfam families were not checked, and they may score poorly against all four target CMs *(inference)*. This matters because mitochondrial off-target binding is where the study has most to say clinically, yet the mitochondrial sample is thin *(project data)*: **9 usable mitochondrial aminoglycoside copies and 16 mitochondrial tetracycline copies** out of 1,179 usable copies. A defensible design treats mitochondria as a case study with per-structure reporting (streptomycin at A1555/C1556, tigecycline, linezolid), not as a third statistical stratum *(inference)*. The *(project data)* class balance is uneven elsewhere too: aminoglycosides contribute 599 usable copies (about 268 bacterial and 322 eukaryotic), tetracyclines 124, macrolides 80 and other classes 16–31 each, so cross-kingdom claims will be strong for aminoglycosides and anecdotal for most other classes.

## Quality, redundancy and protein contacts decide whether reviewers trust the numbers

Ligand models in ribosome structures are not uniformly trustworthy, and the field knows it. Bulkley et al. found their chloramphenicol model "differs substantially from the prevailing model" and that some *D. radiodurans* macrolide models disagree with archaeal and *T. thermophilus* ones, and they attribute the discrepancies to density interpretation rather than species ([Bulkley et al. 2010, PMC2951403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2951403)). Paternoga et al. report that earlier, lower-resolution models of the same classes differ in drug pose and surrounding rRNA conformation from their 1.6–2.2 Å set ([PMC10497419](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10497419)). This is a trap specific to cross-kingdom claims: an apparent species difference can be a model-quality difference *(inference)*. Every copy therefore needs density-fit flags from the wwPDB validation XML (RSCC/RSR for X-ray; Q-score and atom inclusion for cryo-EM), with results reported with and without flagged copies. The EMDataResource ligand challenge concluded that "a composite rather than a single score was needed" ([Lawson et al. 2024, PMC10854310](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10854310/)), cryo-EM ligand Q-scores run systematically lower than X-ray RSCC ([PMC11383698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11383698/)), and no RNA-specific threshold exists, so cut-offs must be calibrated per method. The 2001–2003 *D. radiodurans* macrolide and chloramphenicol entries make a natural positive control: a credible flagging scheme should catch them.

Ions and water need their own class. Mg²⁺ is systematically over-assigned in ribosome models, and only stereochemistry at better than about 2.4 Å reliably distinguishes Mg²⁺ from K⁺ ([Auffinger et al. 2021](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/3/243); [Leonarski et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1148)). Water networks in antibiotic sites are resolvable only near 2 Å ([PMC10497419](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10497419)). Bridged contacts should be restricted to high-resolution entries and never compared directly across resolution bins *(inference)*.

Contact coverage is a concrete, measured risk *(project data)*. PDBe Arpeggio covers only ligand copies in the preferred assembly, and **133 drug copies have no PDBe contacts at all**, mostly cryo-EM entries from 2021 onward. Excluding them would bias the dataset against exactly the recent eukaryotic and mitochondrial structures that make the study cross-kingdom. The study must compute contacts itself, for every copy, with fixed geometry rules, and use PDBe and RCSB annotations as cross-checks with the source recorded per contact *(inference)*.

Redundancy runs along four axes: two 70S copies per *T. thermophilus* asymmetric unit, long near-identical series from single labs, the same drug across species, and several sites per drug. No survey found documents a ribosome-specific deduplication rule ([data notes, DRLiPS and RPocket](https://web.iitm.ac.in/bioinfo2/DRLiPS/Details.html); [RPocket, PMC8424408](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8424408/)). A hierarchical collapse fits our design and our denominator rule *(inference)*: copy → entry → (drug, site, species) → (drug, site), with every count reporting numerator and denominator at its level and the denominator restricted to structures where the position is observed and mapped.

Protein-mediated selectivity is the scope limit that must be stated up front. The cycloheximide resistance of *Candida albicans* is explained by a change in ribosomal protein **eL42**, not rRNA ([HAL hal-03813407](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03813407)), and HARIBOSS++ shows how many ribosomal pockets are mixed RNA/protein ([HARIBOSS++](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.07.749797v1.full)). An rRNA-only axis would miss these determinants *(inference)*. The minimum response is to record protein contacts per copy (Ban nomenclature via the PDBe or RiboXYZ names) and report the fraction of each site's contacts that are protein, even if proteins are not placed on a consensus axis.

Our methylation comparison is the best evidence that position-identity rules are insufficient, and it is a framing asset rather than a nuisance *(project data)*. Across 16 Erm-, Cfr- and NpmA-methylated entries compared with unmethylated twins, the drugs still bind, the ribosome accommodates the methyl by moving the base, and a simple methyl-clash model mispredicts resistance. Together with the 2058 exceptions and tigecycline's site switching, this supports the study's central claim: **selectivity and resistance are structural and contextual, so they need a structural, copy-level comparison on a shared axis**, not a residue lookup.

## Position the study as a quantitative atlas with one sharp biological result

The framing that survives review is "where small molecules contact the ribosome, on one coordinate system, across all kingdoms and all experimental structures, and what differs" *(inference)*. Precedent shows what made comparable work publishable: a reusable curated resource with a web interface (HARIBOSS in Bioinformatics), a clear biological finding (Ekemezie in Cell Reports; Paternoga in NSMB), or careful re-analysis yielding general rules (Leonarski in NAR) ([HARIBOSS](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac483); [Ekemezie et al.](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)00649-7); [Leonarski et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1148)). The study can deliver the first and should aim for one of the others. Because the resource already serves validated Rfam mappings, FR3D pairs and Motif Atlas loops per family, and because PDBe-KB has no RNA aggregation, a PDBe-hosted ribosome drug-site view is a natural NAR Database-issue or Bioinformatics resource paper *(inference)*. A paper led by biology, aimed at NAR's main section, RNA or Structure, needs a headline result. Candidates: the measured fraction of contacted positions that differ between bacterial, cytosolic and mitochondrial ribosomes per drug class; the aminoglycoside secondary sites across kingdoms; or a demonstration that contact sets, not base identity, separate susceptible from resistant ribosomes, using the methylation twins *(inference)*. Reviewers will expect explicit comparison with SPIRAL, HARIBOSS++ and both Melnikov papers, quality and redundancy sensitivity analyses, and a downloadable dataset with provenance.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "novel" means for this project. The databases have already counted ribosome–drug structures, and sequence surveys have already mapped drug-site variation across the tree of life. What nobody has is a reproducible, versioned coordinate frame on which experimental contacts from every kingdom can be compared copy by copy, with denominators that distinguish "not contacted" from "not observed" and "not mapped". That frame is exactly what the aggregated RNA view already builds for single families, which gives the study a technical head start no competitor has.

The most interesting payoff is probably not a new determinant nucleotide. It is likely to be the demonstration that single-residue selectivity rules are the exception: resistant ribosomes that still bind the drug, mitoribosomes with the "bacterial" base, drugs that switch sites with concentration. That argument can only be made with a structural, all-copy, cross-kingdom view. Its credibility depends entirely on the cross-family bridge and on per-copy quality flags, so those two checks should come before any further data collection.
