# Bring metals and water into RNA–ligand contacts

The literature's strongest message for this pipeline is that **excluding ions and water hides the main recognition element for many RNA ligands**. Riboswitches bind phosphate, diphosphate and carboxylate groups (TPP, glmS GlcN6P, NAD+, glycine, glutamine, SAM-V) largely through hydrated Mg2+. The fluoride riboswitch makes no direct RNA contact at all. In high-resolution ribosome antibiotic sites, up to half of all drug–rRNA hydrogen bonds go through water. The rest of the prototype has support in the literature. Interaction fingerprints add information that RMSD lacks. Contacts on Watson–Crick (WC) edges and stacking contacts dominate RNA recognition. As far as these notes show, **no published tool keys fingerprints to Rfam positions or types ligand contacts by Leontis–Westhof edge**, so ECIFP-on-CM-positions and the edge classifier have no direct precedent. Three gaps matter most. First, the quality of each ligand copy and the identity of each ion are often unreliable in deposited models. Second, Arpeggio's atom typing is officially protein-only. Third, no RNA benchmark exists for binding-mode cut-offs or for pocket equivalence across families. The final table ranks eleven improvements. Water/ion bridging with resolution-aware eligibility comes first, then per-copy ligand quality flags, then a versioned additive list.

Throughout, **sourced findings carry citations**, and **statements marked *(inference)* are this report's own reasoning** or design suggestions taken from the research notes.

## Fingerprint tools stop at residues; none uses family coordinates or edges

fingeRNAt is the only interaction-fingerprint tool built for nucleic acids. Each residue gets 12 bits. Ion-mediated contacts take four of them (Mg2+, K+, Na+, other), and one more is water-mediated. A coarse PBS mode records only phosphate, base and sugar contacts. Similarity comes from Tanimoto, Tversky and six other metrics ([fingeRNAt GitHub](https://github.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt); [Szulc et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783)). Its defaults are concrete enough to adopt: **ion bridges at Mg 3.2, Na 3.6, K 3.9 and other ions 3.5 Å**, water ≤ 3.5 Å, H-bond D···A 3.9 Å with angle ≥ 100° ([fingeRNAt config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt/master/code/config.py)). PLIP and ProLIF accept RNA receptors but reuse protein atom typing. PLIP recognises nucleotides only by the names A/U/C/G, so it silently drops modified residues ([PLIP config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pharmai/plip/master/plip/basic/config.py)). **pdbe-arpeggio states it supports "only protein structures in mmCIF format"** and does not handle alt-locs ([PDBe Arpeggio](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio)). ECIFP, which is built on Arpeggio, inherits that typing. The ECIFP preprint itself could not be checked for how it handles RNA.

The tools also disagree on what counts as "phosphate". fingeRNAt puts O3′ and O5′ in the sugar group, not the phosphate group ([config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt/master/code/config.py)). No tool assigns ligand contacts to Leontis–Westhof edges. The only edge-level ligand survey is manual. It covered 67 riboswitch structures and 80 base:ligand pairs, and **the WC edge was most common, then the purine sugar edge, then the uracil Hoogsteen edge** ([Seelam et al. 2019](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/25/10/1274)). In a separate survey, base-specific contacts make up **65% and backbone contacts 35%** of RNA–ligand H-bonds ([Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682)). Both are usable sanity targets for the project's edge classes *(inference)*.

The benchmark evidence on similarity is thin but informative. In fingeRNAt's clustering of 1,508 docked TAR compounds, **dropping lipophilic bits raised the silhouette from 0.516 to 0.72** and separated actives from inactives more clearly. SIFt similarity correlated only modestly with pose RMSD: mean R² was 0.417 over 144 redocked complexes ([Szulc et al. 2022, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9197077/fullTextXML)). Two consequences follow *(inference)*. Arpeggio's low-specificity vdW/proximal contacts probably dilute ECIFP similarity. And interaction similarity is a separate axis from the existing RMSD/TM clustering, not a redundant one. The 0.7–0.85 Tanimoto cut-off often quoted is a chemical-fingerprint heuristic, not an interaction-fingerprint result ([PMC4456712](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4456712/)). Alternatives to Tanimoto for interaction fingerprints have been assessed ([Rácz et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6755604/)).

## Recognition runs through edges, stacks, metals and water

Huang & Lilley's review of all solved riboswitches concludes that most make multiple H-bonds to the ligand and often stack it. Adenosine-containing ligands pair and stack "just like a section of the RNA itself", and **"metal-ion-mediated contacts are frequently found"** ([Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)). The edge used varies between classes for the same ligand. The SAM adenine pairs cis Hoogsteen:WC in SAM-I, trans WC:sugar edge in SAM-III and trans Hoogsteen:WC in SAM-V ([Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)). In the purine riboswitch, specificity comes from WC pairing of the ligand with residue 74 ([Serganov et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018); [Gilbert et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2006.04.003)). Edge-level typing is therefore the right granularity. Recording the full LW family of each ligand pseudo-pair would be more informative than "WC edge contacted" *(inference)*.

Metal bridges are not marginal. The TPP pyrophosphate is held through "bivalent metal ions and water molecules", and the thiazole is not recognised at all ([Serganov et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04740)). GlcN6P's phosphate is coordinated by two hydrated Mg2+ ions ([Cochrane et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2006.12.005)). Glycine recognition uses a pair of Mg2+ ions ([Huang et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2010.11.026)). **F− contacts only three Mg2+ ions**, which in turn bind the phosphates ([Ren et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11152)). The ZTP base is coordinated inner-sphere by Mg2+ ([Trausch et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2015.06.007)). Two further interactions fall outside an H-bond/stacking dichotomy. One is cation–π stacking of guanidinium in guanidine-I/II/III ([Reiss et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2016.11.020)). The other is SAM-V's C=O···S+ electrostatic recognition, which discriminates SAM from SAH ([Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)).

Ribosome antibiotic sites have even more solvent involvement. Cryo-EM of 17 antibiotics at 1.6–2.2 Å resolved ordered waters that are conserved within each scaffold. Kasugamycin makes **10 direct and 10 water-mediated H-bonds**. Streptomycin makes no base-specific contacts. **Tetracycline's only sequence-specific contact is a stack on C1054**, and its other contacts are backbone H-bonds plus Mg1/Mg2 bridges. Mg1 is present without the drug, while Mg2 appears only with it ([Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)). Paromomycin at the A site makes 13 direct H-bonds and 12 water-mediated ones ([Vicens & Westhof 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3)). The water signal depends heavily on resolution. Across 207 complexes, only 15% have any water-mediated RNA–ligand contact, yet at ≤ 1.5 Å **water bridges (74) outnumber direct H-bonds (62)** ([Szulc et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783)). With ions and water excluded, the current pipeline will show TPP, tetracycline or fluoride sites as partial or empty. Any bridging category needs a "solvent modelled" eligibility denominator, so that missing water reads as "not observable" rather than "absent" *(inference, consistent with the project's missing-vs-absent rule)*.

Pocket conformation also differs between ligand-free and bound states. The purine pocket "zippers up" on binding ([Serganov et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018)). The adenine riboswitch has two apo conformers that differ at the junction ([Stagno et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20599)). SAM-II forms its pocket by conformational capture ([Haller et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.562)). The lysine pocket, in contrast, is preorganised with syn nucleotides in both states, and **ligand pockets are enriched in syn, C2′-endo and unpaired nucleotides** ([Kligun & Mandel-Gutfreund 2013](https://doi.org/10.4161/rna.24682)).

## Deposited ligands and ions need per-copy quality flags

Ligand quality has to be judged per copy. In 4FE5, two acetates in the same entry have **RSCC 0.962 and 0.706**, while the hypoxanthine has RSCC 0.945 and RSR 0.072 ([4FE5 validation XML](https://files.wwpdb.org/pub/pdb/validation_reports/fe/4fe5/4fe5_validation.xml.gz)). The current wwPDB X-ray criteria flag a ligand at **RSCC < 0.8 or RSR > 0.4**. LLDF is retired, and fit metrics are unreliable worse than 3 Å resolution or at high B ([wwPDB VR help](https://wwpdb-beta.rcsb.org/validation/2017/XrayValidationReportHelp); [Smart et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318002541)). For cryo-EM, the validation XML carries a Q-score and residue inclusion for each ligand (7K00 paromomycin: Q 0.717, inclusion 0.905). There is no endorsed cut-off ([7K00 validation XML](https://files.wwpdb.org/pub/pdb/validation_reports/k0/7k00/7k00_validation.xml.gz)), and the EMDataResource challenge called for composite scoring ([Lawson et al. 2024](https://emdataresource.org/news/ligand-challenge-publication.html)). **No RNA-specific ligand-quality thresholds exist.** Benchmark-grade filters such as PoseBusters (≤ 2 Å, RSR ≤ 0.2) suit benchmarking ([PoseBusters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05777)) but would remove most riboswitch and ribosome entries *(inference)*.

Additives and ions are the other main hazard. BioLiP2 keeps a citable **list of 463 artefact ligands**. It discards a listed ligand that appears more than 15 times in a structure or binds only two consecutive residues; otherwise it checks the literature ([BioLiP2](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad630)). HARIBOSS deliberately keeps buffers ([HARIBOSS](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/17/4185/6633309)). Triphosphates cannot be excluded generically because real ZTP-class ligands carry them ([RNA suppl.](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/suppl/2023/07/07/rna.079427.122.DC1/Supplemental_Material.pdf)). Ion identities are frequently wrong. Mg2+ is over-assigned where K+ fits better: Mg–O is about 2.1 Å with octahedral geometry, against about 2.8 Å for K–O, and K+ is likely the prevalent first-shell ion in the 6QNR/6SJ6 ribosome models ([Auffinger et al. 2021](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/3/243)). **Mg2+ "near imine nitrogens" (N7) is mostly misinterpreted density** ([Leonarski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkw1175)). Ribosome antibiotic poses have also been disputed between species and structures ([Bulkley et al. 2010](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/4V7X); [Dunkle et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1007988107)).

## Binding-site comparison has no RNA benchmark, and cross-family equivalence comes in two kinds

No RNA analogue of SiteAlign, ProBiS or APoc has been validated. Protein site-comparison benchmarks also overstate performance when the datasets contain similar structures ([Govindaraj & Brylinski 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5845264/)). Pose comparison in the standard approach uses symmetry-corrected ligand RMSD after superposing the pocket. Naive atom matching can be badly wrong (**1.00 Å vs the correct 2.46 Å** on 1DRJ) ([spyrmsd](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7457508/)). Interaction-fingerprint clustering is used to reduce poses to a few modes ([ProLIF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8466659)). No RNA-specific cut-off for RMSD or fingerprint Tanimoto was found.

Published cross-class comparisons describe two different situations. In shared scaffolds with tuned pockets, the ligand binds at homologous positions, sometimes shifted. Examples are purine vs 2′-dG-I/II ([Matyjasik & Batey 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6847200)), Guanine-I vs II ([NAR 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf884)) and ykkC guanidine/ppGpp/PRPP, where a single G96A mutation switches specificity 40,000-fold ([Knappenberger et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/36381)). In convergent recognition on unrelated folds, the same recognition motif appears without positional homology. preQ1-II/III and SAM-II reuse U•A-U triples, reported to superpose at about 1 Å (figures from a search summary) ([Liberman et al. 2015](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1349644)). SAM-I, SAM-II and SAM-III use entirely different folds and different SAM conformations ([Batey review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6061858)). SAM-IV shares positions with SAM-I "despite rearranged structures" ([Weinberg et al. 2008](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/14/5/822.full)), which a CM-to-CM mapping may not capture *(inference)*. Topology-independent aligners can provide an independent check. ARTEMIS is one (Apache-2.0) ([Bohdan et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11472068/)), and US-align has non-sequential modes ([Zhang et al. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004222014900)).

For external cross-checks, **HARIBOSS is the only maintained, CC0, structure-based RNA–ligand set**. It holds 2,131 PDB entries and 5,002 pockets, and its data is versioned on GitHub ([HARIBOSS](https://hariboss.pasteur.cloud/about-general)). It uses a 6 Å interaction radius, while benchmark sets use 4 Å ([RNAsite](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa1092)). HARIBOSS "families" are coarse categories, not Rfam accessions ([HARIBOSS pockets](https://hariboss.pasteur.cloud/about-pocket)). Only DRLiPS groups binding sites by Rfam annotation ([DRLiPS](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf239)). ML benchmarks collapse within-family ensembles, for example all 22 TPP riboswitches into one representative ([RNAsite](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa1092)). That per-family view is the gap this project fills *(inference)*.

## Conclusion

The prototype's core design matches how the literature describes RNA recognition. Typing contacts by edge is the right granularity, because the same ligand pairs through different edges in different classes, and fingerprint similarity adds information that RMSD does not. Keying fingerprints and binding sites to Rfam positions is new; no surveyed tool does it. What the literature changes is the definition of a "contact". For phosphate-, carboxylate- and anion-bearing ligands, and for most ribosome antibiotics, the recognition interface is RNA–metal–water–ligand. A contact map that drops the middle two looks clean but is systematically incomplete. That bias is also confounded by resolution, so the fix needs eligibility denominators as well as new contact types.

The main unknowns are empirical, and this project is well placed to resolve them. There is no RNA benchmark for binding-mode cut-offs, no PDB-wide count of edge usage or of metal-mediated contacts, and no validated test of pocket equivalence across families. Per-family ensembles with per-copy quality flags could produce the first such reference numbers. RF00167 ligand-class separation and the RF00177 antibiotic water and metal counts are the natural first tests.
