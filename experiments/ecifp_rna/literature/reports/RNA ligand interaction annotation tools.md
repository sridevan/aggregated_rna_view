# Pair fingeRNAt and PLIP with Arpeggio

No single tool gives better RNA–ligand annotations than PDBe-Arpeggio on every point. The best choice is to **keep PDBe-Arpeggio as the atom-level backbone and benchmark it against fingeRNAt and PLIP**, the only two open tools that treat a nucleic acid explicitly as the receptor. fingeRNAt is also the only tool found with ion-specific Mg²⁺/Na⁺/K⁺-mediated and water-mediated ligand–RNA bridges ([fingeRNAt](https://github.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt)). Mol\* is a fourth, permissively licensed engine. It already types nucleotide phosphates as anions, but it has no supported batch interface ([Mol\* charged.ts](https://github.com/molstar/molstar/blob/master/packages/model/src/props/computed/interactions/charged.ts)). This matters because Arpeggio's own documentation says it supports "only protein structures in mmCIF format". Its curated atom-type overrides cover the 20 amino acids only, so every nucleotide atom in the project's ECIFP, edge and SAM analyses is typed by generic OpenBabel SMARTS on bonds perceived from geometry ([PDBe-Arpeggio README](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/README.md); [interactions.py](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/arpeggio/core/interactions.py)). None of the surveyed tools ships a curated nucleotide atom table or assigns contacts to Leontis–Westhof edges. Whichever engine is chosen, the project therefore still needs its own A/C/G/U (and parent-CCD) atom→donor/acceptor/edge table. **No published head-to-head benchmark of interaction tools on RNA–ligand complexes exists**, so the benchmark proposed here would be the first. The closest precedent is a 40-complex protein study in which tools agreed well on H-bonds and aromatic contacts but disagreed most on water bridges ([Kokh et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11066)). The recommended design uses a tiered reference: hand-curated author-reported contacts, multi-tool consensus, and stereochemistry-checked ions. It reports per-type precision/recall and tool–tool Jaccard, and adds a hydrogenation-sensitivity arm and an ion-identity arm, stratified by method, resolution, ligand class and solvent modelling.

Throughout, statements marked *(inference)* are this report's reasoning rather than a sourced finding.

## Arpeggio's RNA weaknesses sit in typing, solvent and coverage, not in output format

PDBe-Arpeggio is a strong baseline in one respect. Its JSON already gives atom-level partners (`auth_asym_id`, `auth_seq_id`, `auth_atom_id`, `label_comp_id`), a list of contact types and a distance. Atom–plane, plane–plane and group records are separate ([README](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/README.md)). Its contact vocabulary is also the richest of the candidates: clash, covalent, vdW, proximal, H-bond, **weak H-bond (CH···O)**, polar, weak polar, halogen, ionic, metal, aromatic, hydrophobic, carbonyl, plus cation–π, donor–π and carbon–π atom–plane classes ([README](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/README.md)). Mapping atoms to WC, Hoogsteen and sugar edges is easy from this output.

The weaknesses lie elsewhere. **Atom typing**: `config.py` lists the standard nucleotides, but no nucleotide atom-type dictionary exists. Bonds come from `ConnectTheDots()` and `PerceiveBondOrders()` plus `_struct_conn`, not from CCD bond orders ([protein_reader.py](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/arpeggio/core/protein_reader.py); [config.py](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/arpeggio/core/config.py)). This is fragile for modified nucleotides and for poorly resolved bases *(inference)*. **Solvent**: water appears only as atom–water contacts (SELECTION_WATER and related classes), with every water atom typed as both donor and acceptor. There are no water or ion bridges, only a `metal` contact type ([interactions.py](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/arpeggio/core/interactions.py)). **Structure handling**: the reader trims to the first model and keeps every altloc atom, and the README warns that altlocs and missing density "may result in anomalous results" ([protein_reader.py](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/arpeggio/core/protein_reader.py); [README](https://github.com/PDBeurope/arpeggio/blob/master/README.md)). **Coverage of the API service**: project experience is that PDBe computes Arpeggio contacts only for ligand copies in the preferred assembly. This is project knowledge, not a cited source. Copies outside it must read as "not annotated by source".

The solvent gap is the most consequential one biologically. TPP's pyrophosphate is held through "bivalent metal ions and water molecules" ([Serganov et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04740)). In 16S, paromomycin at the A site makes 13 direct and 12 water-mediated H-bonds ([Vicens & Westhof 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3)), and kasugamycin makes 10 direct and 10 water-mediated ones ([Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)). An Arpeggio-only contact map shows these sites as partial *(inference)*.

The candidates split into four groups. Nucleic-acid-aware profilers are fingeRNAt and PLIP. Viewer engines are Mol\*. Generic fingerprint toolkits are ProLIF, LUNA, ODDT, BINANA and GetContacts. RNA-centric resources do not type ligand contacts at all.

**fingeRNAt is the most RNA-appropriate engine but the least production-ready.** Its ion bridges use a ligand N/O/S–ion ≤ X and nucleic acid N/O–ion ≤ X rule, with X = 3.2 Å for Mg, 3.9 Å for K, 3.6 Å for Na and 3.5 Å otherwise. These are reported as separate Mg-, K-, Na- and Other-mediated classes ([implementation.md](https://github.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt/blob/master/implementation.md)). Water bridges are distance-only, at ≤ 3.5 Å on both sides. Its own data show how much hydrogenation matters. Over 207 RNA–ligand complexes, H-bond methods that use hydrogen positions found **almost three times fewer H-bonds (1,735–1,822) than the default heavy-atom method (5,026)**, and the count depended on which program placed the hydrogens ([Szulc et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783)). Its PDB-only input means each 16S entry needs a cropped, renumbered PDB extract around the ligand *(inference)*. It has had no commits since mid-2022 ([GitHub](https://github.com/n-szulc/fingeRNAt)). The licence also needs checking: the repository reports GPL-3.0, but a Zenodo record lists Apache-2.0 ([Zenodo 7486222](https://zenodo.org/records/7486222/latest)).

**PLIP is the best-maintained independent comparator.** It has an atom-level XML report, a curated one-water bridge geometry (water–polar atom 2.5–4.1 Å, ω 71–140°, θ ≥ 100°) and metal-complex geometry fitting ([config.py](https://github.com/pharmai/plip/blob/master/plip/basic/config.py); [DOCUMENTATION](https://github.com/pharmai/plip/blob/master/DOCUMENTATION.md)). Its RNA support is shallow, though. The 2021 paper demonstrated a drug bound to DNA and an RNA bound to a protein, not a small molecule bound to RNA ([Adasme et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkab294)). Charged groups are documented for protein residues only, and modified nucleotides fall outside its A/C/G/U name list ([config.py](https://github.com/pharmai/plip/blob/master/plip/basic/config.py)). Whether `--dnareceptor` treats phosphate oxygens as salt-bridge anions was not verified.

**The RNA-specific resources do not compete as contact engines.** DSSR reports atom-level H-bonds in JSON, but by default only within nucleic acids. Its free Basic tier is academic-only and the Pro tier is commercial, which suits a public pipeline poorly ([x3dna.org](https://x3dna.org/highlights/get-hydrogen-bonds-with-dssr); [CTV licence](https://inventions.techventures.columbia.edu/licenses/dssr-basic-academic-1-seat-now--188)). The RNApolis annotator covers base pairs, stacking and base–phosphate/ribose contacts, with no ligand handling ([PyPI RNApolis](https://pypi.org/project/RNApolis)). HARIBOSS, BioLiP2, rnaglib and the RNAsite benchmark sets provide complexes, pockets or residue labels, not typed atom contacts ([HARIBOSS](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.05.17.492306.full); [BioLiP2](https://seq2fun.dcmb.med.umich.edu/BioLiP2/download.html); [rnaglib](https://github.com/cgoliver/rnaglib)). Edge-level ligand typing exists only as a manual survey: **80 base:ligand pairs across 67 riboswitch structures, with the Watson–Crick edge most common, then the purine sugar edge, then the uracil Hoogsteen edge** ([Seelam et al. 2019](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/25/10/1274.full)). The project's Leontis–Westhof edge classifier therefore has no tool-level precedent; it can only be validated against that survey *(inference)*.

## Shortlist three engines and two reference resources

The shortlist follows from three needs: RNA-aware typing, solvent and ion bridges, and atom-level output for edge mapping.

**PDBe-Arpeggio stays as the primary engine**, run locally from the pip package on full mmCIF so that every ligand copy is covered, not only preferred-assembly copies. Add a project-side nucleotide atom-type override table, and add derived water/ion bridges built from its atom–water and metal contacts *(inference)*. Its JSON contract, weak-H-bond class and production use at PDBe make it the cheapest base to extend, and its GPL-3.0 licence is acceptable for running server-side.

**fingeRNAt is the RNA-native comparator** and the reference implementation for ion- and water-mediated bridges. It is used on cropped PDB extracts, accepting that it may need small patches given the 2022 code freeze.

**PLIP is the second independent comparator**, actively maintained, with angular water-bridge criteria and an atom-level XML report.

**Mol\*** is a conditional fourth candidate. Its MIT licence and the shared viewer make it attractive for showing the same contacts in the browser, but running it headless and serialising its output are undocumented ([Mol\* interactions extension](https://github.com/molstar/molstar/blob/master/apps/docs/src/content/docs/v5/extensions/interactions.md)). It belongs in a later phase. **ProLIF** is a candidate only if explicit-hydrogen inputs are prepared anyway. It is well maintained and Apache-2.0, but it has no documented RNA path ([ProLIF tutorial](https://prolif.readthedocs.io/en/stable/notebooks/pdb.html)). LUNA, ODDT, BINANA and GetContacts are dropped: they have no nucleic-acid typing, and GetContacts collapses stacking onto a single protein CG atom ([GetContacts README](https://github.com/getcontacts/getcontacts/blob/master/README.md)). DSSR is excluded on licence grounds.

The two reference resources are **HARIBOSS** and **the Seelam 2019 riboswitch base:ligand catalogue**. HARIBOSS gives a curated complex set of 2,131 entries and 5,002 pockets; one note records its data as CC0, another found no licence shown, so this needs checking before reuse ([HARIBOSS](https://hariboss.pasteur.cloud/about-general)). The Seelam catalogue is the only edge-level ligand truth ([Seelam et al. 2019](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/25/10/1274.full)). **CheckMyMetal** and the Auffinger ion stereochemistry criteria serve as ion-identity references ([CheckMyMetal 2024](https://journals.iucr.org/m/issues/2024/05/00/be5298/index.html); [Auffinger et al. 2021](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/3/243.full)).

## A benchmark needs tiered truth because no gold standard exists

Published practice is thin. PLIP was validated pass/fail on 30 protein–ligand complexes, by checking that every interaction the original papers reported was detected ([PLIP 2015](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/43/W1/W443/2467865)). Kokh et al. scored a new tool against a consensus of PLIP, FLIP, LPC and MOE on 40 complexes, excluding hydrophobic contacts "since their definition is not as clear cut". They found 5 false positives and 6 false negatives in 250 non-hydrophobic interactions. Of 69 water bridges, though, 23 were found by none of the other tools. The authors blamed differing H-bond parameters and "the ambiguity of the assignment of hydrogen orientation" ([Kokh et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11066)). Errington et al. used ProLIF crystal interactions as the reference for a recovery-rate metric. They noted that results are "very sensitive to the protonation state", which decides whether a contact is labelled ionic or H-bond ([Errington et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.20227)). Tool defaults also disagree on basic definitions: an H-bond can be H···A ≤ 3.0 Å, or D···A ≤ 3.5 or ≤ 4.1 Å, and "there is usually no consensus" ([ProLIF 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-021-00548-6)).

**Reference truth should therefore come in three tiers** *(inference, following the PLIP and Kokh precedents)*. Tier A is hand-curated atom-level contacts from the primary papers of about 30–40 complexes, each claim citing its paper. It is recall-oriented: it tests whether a tool recovers what experts considered meaningful, but it cannot measure precision for weak contacts. Tier B is a majority consensus of the shortlisted tools on a larger set. It is always reported beside Tier A, because tools sharing OpenBabel typing share its biases. Tier C is ion sites judged by stereochemistry. The Auffinger windows are Mg²⁺–O about 2.06 Å (1.9–2.4 Å) with octahedral geometry, Na⁺–O about 2.41 Å, and K⁺–O about 2.8 Å (2.6–3.2 Å). Distances of 2.4–2.6 Å fit neither ion ([Auffinger et al. 2021](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/3/243.full)). Mg²⁺ next to nucleobase N7 is mostly misread density ([Leonarski et al.](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/25/2/173)). The size of the problem is large: in ribosome 6QNR, **853 of 1,005 Mg²⁺ fail at least one criterion** ([Auffinger et al. 2021](https://rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/3/243.full)).

**Metrics** *(inference)*. Compare at two granularities. The atom-pair level (ligand atom, RNA atom, type) is needed for edges; the residue level (nucleotide, type) is what fingerprints use. For each type, report precision, recall and F1 against Tier A and against Tier B, plus pairwise Jaccard and Fleiss' κ across tools. All of this goes through a harmonised type map, because Arpeggio "ionic", PLIP "salt bridge" and fingeRNAt "cation–anion" are the same thing. Because protonation flips labels, score a merged polar/electrostatic class first and the split classes second. Analyse proximal, vdW and hydrophobic contacts separately, as Kokh did. Add an edge-level score: the fraction of Seelam's 80 pairs whose edge the pipeline assigns correctly.

**Cutoff sensitivity.** Sweep the main thresholds, for example H-bond D···A from 3.2 to 4.1 Å reported in a ≤ 3.5 Å band and a 3.5–4.1 Å band, and stacking as centroid ≤ 5.5 Å with offset ≤ 2.0 Å versus a distance-only rule. Report how many contacts change type *(inference)*.

**Hydrogenation sensitivity.** No RNA-specific hydrogenation method exists ([Szulc et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783)). The primary run should feed every tool one common hydrogenated input: RNA hydrogens from Reduce, which supports nucleic acids and optimises OH and NH flips ([Reduce README](https://genomics-lab.fleming.gr/fleming/tools/molprobity/modules/reduce/README.usingReduce.txt)), and ligand hydrogens and charges from the CCD. A sensitivity arm then reruns with each tool's native OpenBabel hydrogens and with Hydride ([Hydride](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8966362/)), and reports per-type Jaccard against the common run as the non-determinism floor. Errington's PDB2PQR + RDKit + hydrogen-only minimisation pipeline is the protein precedent ([Errington et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.20227)).

**Ion handling.** Run three ion modes: deposited identities with fingeRNAt cutoffs; Auffinger inner-sphere windows with an explicit inner- versus outer-sphere split; and CheckMyMetal-flagged sites kept but marked "identity questionable". This follows the project's flag-don't-drop rule *(inference)*. CheckMyMetal now accepts cryo-EM maps, but cryo-EM scores run lower than X-ray at the same resolution ([CheckMyMetal 2024](https://journals.iucr.org/m/issues/2024/05/00/be5298/index.html)).

**Stratification.** Stratify by method (X-ray / cryo-EM / NMR); by resolution (≤ 2.0, 2.0–2.5, 2.5–3.0, > 3.0 Å, where 2.4 Å is the ion-assignment boundary); by ligand class; by whether waters and ions are modelled; and by ligand altloc or partial occupancy. Use bootstrap confidence intervals and per-family as well as pooled results, so the many purine structures do not dominate *(inference)*. The water stratum is essential. Only 15% of 207 complexes have any water-mediated contact, yet at ≤ 1.5 Å water bridges outnumber direct H-bonds (74 vs 62) ([Szulc et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009783)). Bridge recall must therefore use a "solvent modelled" denominator, not all chains.

## A four-family benchmark plan

The plan below is a design proposal *(inference)*. It reuses the project's existing chain sets and mapping to Rfam positions and limits Tier A to complexes whose primary papers describe contacts explicitly.

| Family / site | Tier A expectations from literature | What it stresses |
|---|---|---|
| **Purine RF00167** | Ligand WC-pairs with residue 74, giving specificity ([Serganov et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2004.11.018); [Gilbert et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2006.04.003)); per-copy ligand quality varies within one entry (4FE5 acetates RSCC 0.962 vs 0.706) ([4FE5 validation](https://files.wwpdb.org/pub/pdb/validation_reports/fe/4fe5/4fe5_validation.xml.gz)) | Edge assignment precision; planar-ligand stacking definitions; additive vs ligand handling; family dominance in pooled stats |
| **SAM classes (SAM-I RF00162 and others)** | The same adenine pairs cis Hoogsteen:WC in SAM-I, trans WC:sugar in SAM-III and trans Hoogsteen:WC in SAM-V; SAM-V discriminates SAM from SAH by C=O···S⁺ electrostatics ([Huang & Lilley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033583525100012)) | Full LW-family assignment of ligand pseudo-pairs; cation/sulfonium electrostatics that no tool types as such |
| **TPP RF00059** | Pyrophosphate held through bivalent metals and water; thiazole not recognised ([Serganov et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04740)) | Ion-mediated bridge recall; Mg identity checks; apo (8F4O) vs holo denominators |
| **16S rRNA (RF00177) antibiotic sites** | Paromomycin 13 direct + 12 water-mediated H-bonds ([Vicens & Westhof 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0969-2126(01)00629-3)); kasugamycin 10 + 10; tetracycline's only sequence-specific contact is a C1054 stack, plus Mg1/Mg2 bridges ([Paternoga et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01047-y)); 7K00 paromomycin Q-score 0.717 ([7K00 validation](https://files.wwpdb.org/pub/pdb/validation_reports/k0/7k00/7k00_validation.xml.gz)) | Water-bridge recall; ribosome-scale mmCIF (crop for PDB-only tools); ion misassignment (6QNR); modified nucleotides at drug sites; cryo-EM strata |

The execution order is as follows. **First**, build Tier A, roughly 8–10 complexes per family, from the papers above. **Second**, prepare one cropped, hydrogenated input per ligand copy (a 10–12 Å sphere, label_asym_id/label_seq_id kept in a sidecar map so outputs return to Rfam positions), and record CCD, validation metrics and solvent-modelled flags as provenance. **Third**, run local PDBe-Arpeggio (all copies), fingeRNAt (with water and ion bridges and `-detail`) and PLIP (`--dnareceptor`, `--nohydro` on the common input), and compare local Arpeggio with the PDBe API output to measure the preferred-assembly gap. **Fourth**, score per type, per tier, per stratum, and score edges against Seelam's catalogue. **Fifth**, run the hydrogenation, cutoff-sweep and ion-mode arms. A tool earns adoption for a contact class only if it beats Arpeggio's recall on Tier A for that class without losing precision against Tier B. On the evidence so far, fingeRNAt's bridges are the most likely to qualify.

## Conclusion

The question is less which tool to adopt than which layer to own. Every candidate engine treats nucleotides as an afterthought: at best phosphates become anions and A/C/G/U names are hard-coded. The scientifically decisive parts, curated nucleotide atom typing, Leontis–Westhof edge assignment, ion identity, and eligibility denominators for solvent, therefore have to live in the project regardless of engine. That reframes the external tools as interchangeable contact detectors to be cross-validated, not as sources of RNA expertise.

The benchmark itself would be a publishable contribution. No RNA–ligand tool comparison, threshold sweep or hydrogenation-sensitivity study exists. The four families span the main recognition regimes: edge pairing (purine), class-specific edges and electrostatics (SAM), metal bridging (TPP), and water-dominated cryo-EM sites (16S). They are a compact test bed whose results would generalise beyond this resource. The main uncertainties to resolve early are fingeRNAt's real licence and maintainability, PLIP's phosphate typing in `--dnareceptor` mode, and whether HARIBOSS data can be reused.
