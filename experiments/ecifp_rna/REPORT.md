# ECIFP for RNA–ligand complexes: feasibility check (2026-10-23)

**Sources**
- **Paper:** Kunnakkattu et al., "Large-scale analysis of ligand binding mode similarities in the PDB using interaction fingerprints", bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.17.719144. Only the abstract could be read; the full text returned HTTP 429.
- **Code:** github.com/PDBeurope/ecifp at commit a21de95 (2026-06-11), CC0.

**What was done.** `ecifp_rna.py` reproduces ecifp's fingerprint on our riboswitch families. It adds a binding-site step on Rfam consensus positions. Outputs are in `out/`.

**Environment and caching**
- The environment is an isolated one in the session scratchpad: RDKit 2026.09.1 and pdbeccdutils 2.0.0.
- PDBe API responses and CCD component files were cached there and not kept.

## How ECIFP works (from the code)

### Contacts
- **Source:** the PDBe Arpeggio contacts per ligand copy, from `bound_ligand_interactions/{pdb}/{chain}/{num}`.
- **Contact types:** proximal, clash, vdw_clash and vdw are dropped.
- **Other residues dropped:** water and the other components of the same bound molecule.

### Fingerprints
- **Ligand fingerprint:** a Morgan/ECFP fingerprint (radius 2, 1024 bits) of the ligand's PDBe CCD definition. Only the bits generated by the ligand atoms that make contacts are kept.
- **Polymer fingerprint:** the same construction for each contacted residue type (its CCD definition and its contacting atoms). The residue types are then merged by `chem_comp_id`. This makes the fingerprint position-free: it encodes which chemical fragments touch the ligand, not which residue numbers.

### Similarity and clustering
- **Similarity:** Tanimoto(ligand)^0.4 × Tanimoto(polymer)^0.6.
- **Binding sites:** a separate step. Each site is the set of UniProt residue numbers contacting a ligand, compared by the overlap coefficient and clustered by single linkage at a distance of 0.4.
- **Binding modes:** minimax linkage on 1 − ECIFP, with the cut placed at the largest gap between 0.3 and 0.6.

## Does it work for RNA?

**Inputs.** The inputs exist for RNA and the code does not depend on proteins:
- PDBe Arpeggio contacts are returned for RNA–ligand complexes, with atom-level detail on the nucleotides. Examples: ADE in 1Y26, HPA in 4FE5, TPP in 2GDI.
- Nucleotides, standard and modified, are CCD components, so their fingerprints are built exactly like amino acids'.

**Coverage.** Every non-ion, non-water ligand copy in our data received contacts:

|---|---|---|
| RF00167 | 71 | 71 |

## Limitations for RNA

1. **Small chemical vocabulary.**
   - RNA has 4 standard residues, against 20 amino acids, and they share the ribose-phosphate backbone. So RNA-side fingerprints from different pockets overlap more, and similarity values sit higher.
   - Median RNA-side bits per copy: 42, 45 and 56 in the three families.
   - Distinct RNA-side fingerprints: 50 of 71, 26 of 28 and 13 of 27.
   - The 0.62 cross-pocket score above shows the effect. ECIFP cut-offs tuned on proteins (0.3–0.6) would need re-tuning on RNA.
2. **Position-free by design.** Merging by residue type means that "two U bases contacting through the Watson–Crick edge" looks the same wherever the Us are. For a family view we already have the positions, so the Rfam-position site sets and existing per-position contact tracks carry that information. ECIFP adds the chemistry of the contacting fragments.
3. **Assembly copies.** Arpeggio contacts are computed on the preferred assembly, with generated chain ids such as `A_2`. Ligand copies outside the preferred assembly have no contacts. These are the same constraints already noted for PDBe ligand contacts.
4. **Ions and water.** These are dropped, as in ecifp. In RNA, Mg²⁺ and water often bridge the ligand to the RNA (e.g. the TPP pyrophosphate). Such bridged contacts are therefore invisible to the fingerprint.
5. **Not validated against an RNA benchmark.** The paper's benchmark is protein binding sites. No RNA equivalent was run here; the groupings above are only plausibility checks.

## Possible use in this project

**A binding-mode view in the Ligands tab.** Per family, group ligand copies by ECIFP (similar ligands, similar contacts), and place sites on Rfam positions with the ecifp site rule. Everything needed is either already available or can be fetched:
- PDBe Arpeggio contacts;
- CCD files;
- our mappings.

**Cross-family comparisons.** Compare the same ligand across riboswitch classes, for example SAM in SAM-I, SAM-II and SAM-III, once those families are added.

## Follow-up: do the purine-riboswitch ligands share one pose? (`purine_poses.py`, `out/RF00167_poses.json`)

**Method**
- Each of the 66 main-site copies is superposed onto the 1Y26 X pocket, with ADE 90 as the reference ligand.
- The superposition uses C1′, C4′ and P of the 15 Rfam positions whose C4′ lies within 12 Å of the ligand: 25–29, 51, 53–57 and 79–82.
- Coordinates (model 1) are streamed from the PDBe ModelServer and not stored.
- Ring atoms are taken from the CCD definition with SMARTS: the purine core N1…N9, with C or N allowed at the ring positions, or else a 6-membered ring.
- A flipped pose is tested with the core relabelled by the 180° in-plane flip (N1↔N3, N7↔N9).

**Purine cores (55 copies)**
- Core RMSD to adenine in 1Y26: ≤ 0.5 Å in 38 copies, 0.5–0.8 Å in 8 and > 0.8 Å in 9.
- The flipped labelling never fits better (1.5–2.0 Å throughout). So no ligand binds with the ring turned over.
- The 9 copies above 0.8 Å are 3DS7 GNG, 6P2H GNG, 9LKF GMP, 9NBC OXG, 9NDD A–D OXG and 5SWE ADE. These are about 1 Å translations in the same orientation.
  - GNG and GMP are a nucleoside and a nucleotide, so they carry a ribose or phosphate beyond the purine.
  - The pocket itself differs in 6P2H (pocket RMSD 3.4 Å over 36 atoms).
  - The 9NBC and 9NDD pocket fits are 0.9–1.3 Å, with 42 atoms.

**Other ligands**
- **Non-purine six-membered rings (9 copies):** the ring centre lies 0.2–0.75 Å from adenine's six-membered ring for 3AY, 3AW, ZZS, 29G, 29H and 7PD. It is 1.0–1.4 Å for 5AZ, NPR and H4B.
- **ZZR:** no ring was matched. Its centroid is 0.5 Å from adenine's.
- **5SWD A ADE:** the ModelServer query returned no ligand atoms, and the pocket fit is 4.5 Å. It was not evaluated.

**Interpretation**
- Essentially one pose: the heterocycle occupies the same place and orientation in the pocket. Substituents and the pocket's own conformation account for the larger deviations.
- The ECIFP groups (adenine-like, guanine-like, hypoxanthine/xanthine) therefore reflect chemistry: which ligand atoms and which pocket nucleotide (U or C at Rfam 80) make the contacts. They are not different poses. ECIFP measures contact chemistry, not geometry.

## Follow-up: within a clan (`clan_cross.py`, `out/clan/`)

**Method**
- **Building the sibling chains:** each sibling family's PDBe-mapped chains were built as in the pipeline, with chain coordinates from the ModelServer held in a scratch cache.
- **Aligning:** the chains were aligned with cmalign to the main family's CM, giving positions on the main family's numbering, and to their own CM for comparison.
- **Clustering:** ligand copies of both families were then clustered together, so sites are defined on the main family's positions.
- **Checking the correspondence in 3D:** each sibling chain's pocket was superposed on the main reference pocket through the cross-mapped positions, using the C1′, C4′ and P atoms of main positions within 12 Å of the reference ligand. The reference ligands are 1Y26 X ADE 90 and 2GIS A SAM 301.

### Purine clan: RF00167 + 2dG-I (RF01510; 8 entries, 16 chains)

**Fit of the cross alignment.** On the purine CM, 2dG-I scores 9.8–12.0 bits (average PP 0.90). On its own CM it scores 74.7–75.8 bits (PP 0.998). All 64 aligned nucleotides are matches, and the median PP at the pocket positions is 0.975.

**3D check.** The pocket RMSD is 2.2–2.7 Å over 14 of 15 positions (RF00167 chains are mostly 0.2–0.5 Å). By region, from SS_cons, comparing 3SKZ A with 1Y26 X:

| Region | Positions | C4′ deviation | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| J3/1 and P1 | 79–82 | 0.2–1.8 Å | Correspond |
| J2/3, 3′ part | 54–57 | 0.6–3 Å | Roughly correspond |
| J1/2 | 26–28 | — | The nearest 2dG-I nucleotide is the one mapped to the previous position, at 2.3–3.4 Å. This suggests a one-nucleotide register difference. |
| J2/3, 5′ part | 51–53 | 4–6.5 Å | No 2dG-I nucleotide within 5 Å of the purine riboswitch position: no structural equivalent. **Corrected by the US-align follow-up below:** this was an artefact of the CM-based superposition; 51–53 do correspond. |

So the cross mapping is reliable on one side of the pocket and not on the other. This fits Rfam treating 2dG-I as a separate family.

**Ligands**
- **2′-deoxyguanosine and nucleotides:** the 2dG-I copies are GNG ×7, GMP, DGP and 5GP. They fall in the purine main site, with site overlap 0.71–0.88 with the RF00167 copies, and form one ECIFP mode with the RF00167 GNG ×3 and GMP copies. The best cross-family scores are 0.83–0.96 for GNG, 0.79 for GMP, 0.72 for DGP and 0.74 for 5GP.
- **The other RF00167 groups:** adenine-like, guanine/hypoxanthine and others stay separate from these.
- **SIN in 2dG-I (4 copies):** these contact only 1–2 positions (54–55 or 31). They are separate from everything (ECIFP ≤ 0.10).

### SAM clan: SAM-I (RF00162) + SAM-IV (RF00634; 4 entries) + SAM-I/IV (RF01725; 2 entries)

**Fit of the cross alignment**

| Family | On the SAM-I CM | On its own CM |
|---|---|---|
| SAM-IV | −6.5 bits, 55 of 119 nucleotides as local-end skips | 106 bits |
| SAM-I/IV | 7.3–9.3 bits, 29–36 local-end | 42.6–43.7 bits |

The whole-molecule fit is poor, especially for SAM-IV.

**3D check.**
- Ten of the 14 pocket positions are mapped in every sibling chain; positions 102–105 fall in the skipped stretches.
- The pocket RMSD is 0.57 Å for both SAM-I/IV chains, 0.62 Å for SAM-IV 6UET and 1.24 Å for SAM-IV 6UES.
- So the SAM-binding core corresponds even where the rest of the molecule does not align.

**Ligands.**
- **One site and one mode:** the SAM-IV copy (6UET) and the two SAM-I/IV copies (4L81, 4OQU) fall in the SAM-I site and in the single SAM-I mode.
- **Best cross-family ECIFP:** 0.90, 0.96 and 1.00.

### Reading

**ECIFP itself.** It works across a clan without any correspondence: the same ligand in sibling families scores 0.8–1.0. This is the same chemistry, contacted the same way.

**Site assignment.** This needs a correspondence. The CM cross alignment gives one where the pocket is conserved (the SAM clan). It gives only part of one where the sibling's pocket is rebuilt (2dG-I J1/2 and J2/3). The 3D pocket check is needed to tell the two apart, so a cross-family site should be reported with it, and positions failing it flagged.

**A per-position check.** The local-end skips and the per-position C4′ deviation give a per-position reliability measure. That is more useful than the whole-molecule bit score: SAM-IV has −6.5 bits overall but a 0.6 Å pocket.

## Follow-up: which nucleotide edge contacts the ligand (`base_edges.py`, `out/<ACC>_edges.json`)

**Classification.** Each Arpeggio contact with a family nucleotide is classified by nucleotide part. The atom table follows the Leontis–Westhof edges and is a draft for review:

| Part | Atoms |
|---|---|
| Watson-Crick (W) | A N1; G N1; C N3; U N3 |
| Hoogsteen (H) | A/G N7, C8; C/U C5, C6 |
| Sugar edge (S) | A/G N3; O2′ |
| W/H corner | A N6; G O6; C N4; U O4 |
| W/S corner | A C2; G N2; C/U O2 |
| Base face | Arpeggio ring-centroid (π/stacking) contacts |
| Ribose | C1′ C2′ C3′ C4′ O4′ C5′ |
| Phosphate | P OP1 OP2 OP3 O5′ O3′ |

**Rules**
- A base edge (including O2′ for the sugar edge) is assigned only from hydrogen-bond-type contacts (hbond, polar, ionic, metal_complex). Weak CH contacts are not used for edges.
- Ribose and phosphate are counted from any contact that passes the ECIFP filter (anything except proximal, clash, vdw_clash and vdw). Backbone contacts are often hydrophobic or CH contacts against the ribose, so a hydrogen-bond rule would miss them. This rule was agreed on 2026-10-23. It restores, for example:
  - TPP phosphates at positions 61, 62 and 77 (11–12 of 28 copies);
  - SAM-I ribose at 7, 71 and 104 (25–26 of 27).
- Ion-mediated contacts (e.g. through Mg²⁺) remain invisible, because ions are excluded as ligands.
- A corner atom counts as a corner only when no single-edge atom of the same base was contacted.
- Modified nucleotides use their parent base (a small parent table).

**Reading.** Edges turn the position list into a contact profile, e.g. "position 80 by its Watson-Crick edge; position 26 by sugar edge and face".
- The profiles differ between sites in an interpretable way: base-edge recognition in the riboswitch pockets, backbone-dominated contacts at the tetracycline site.
- ECIFP's Morgan bits already respond to which atoms touch (N1 and N7 have different environments), but not in a readable form.
- Two ways to add edges:
  - **Position × part profile:** a vector of Rfam position × {W, H, S, W/H, W/S, face, ribose, phosphate} per copy. It can be compared within a family, or across a clan through the cross mapping, and shown directly as a track or heatmap.
  - **Position-free features:** features such as `U|W|hbond`, added to the RNA side of ECIFP so cross-family comparison keeps edge information.

## Follow-up: US-align structural correspondence vs CM correspondence (`usalign_check.py`, `out/clan/usalign_check.json`)

**Tool.** US-align was run from github.com/pylelab/USalign, commit 23f14ff (2026-10-19), built with `g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -ffast-math`. Its licence is permissive with attribution. Cite: Zhang et al., Nat Methods 2022; Zhang, Freddolino & Zhang, Nat Protoc 2026.

**Method**
- Each chain is taken at model 1, polymer ATOM residues only. US-align reads no HETATM, so the 5′-GTP and the 3′-cyclic C of 2dG-I were left out.
- Pairwise alignment uses `-mol RNA` and C3′ atoms, against the reference chains 1Y26 X and 2GIS A.
- A multiple structure alignment (`-mm 4`) was run over each set.
- For each main-family position, the US-align partner was compared with the CM cross-mapping partner. Distances are C3′–C3′ after the US-align superposition.

**Same-family sanity checks.**
- 4FE5 B vs 1Y26 X: 66 positions agree, with no disagreements.
- 4KQY vs 2GIS: 89 agree, 2 differ.

### 2dG-I (3SKI A, 3SKL A) vs purine 1Y26 X
TM-score is 0.49 by length normalisation; the multiple alignment scores 0.56.

| Region | Positions | Agreement with the CM mapping |
|---|---|---|
| J2/3 | 51–57 | Agree; 1.2–2.7 Å |
| J3/1 and P1 | 79–82 | Agree; 1.0–2.1 Å |
| J1/2 | 26–28 | Differ by exactly one nucleotide. US-align puts A30 at 26 (0.5 Å); the CM puts C31 there. 29 is matched only by US-align (G34, 3.4 Å) and 25 only by the CM. |
| P2 / L2 | 30–48 | The CM is one nucleotide later throughout. US-align distances are 1.4–3.3 Å in the helix and 4–6 Å in the L2 loop, which differs. |
| P3 loop | 65–75 | Differ as well |

**This corrects an earlier conclusion.** The clan section above said J2/3 51–53 has "no structural equivalent". That came from a superposition through the CM-mapped positions, which included the shifted J1/2 and P2 positions and so distorted the fit. With the US-align superposition, 51 and 53 correspond (2.7 and 1.7 Å) and agree with the CM. Only J1/2 (and P2) carry a register difference.

### SAM-IV (6UET, 6UES) and SAM-I/IV (4L81, 4OQU) vs SAM-I 2GIS A
TM-score is 0.38–0.47, near or below the 0.45 same-topology level for RNA.

**Pocket positions 7, 8, 46–49 and 69–72** agree with the CM for 6UET, 4L81 and 4OQU.

**Positions 102–105** fall in the CM's skipped stretches (local ends), so the CM leaves them unmapped. US-align pairs them:
- SAM-I/IV: 0.2–0.7 Å, so the structural alignment recovers pocket positions the CM missed;
- SAM-IV 6UET: 1.3–4.3 Å.

**6UES pairwise is an outlier.** It is the same sequence as 6UET. Its pairwise alignment is one nucleotide off across P1 (positions 4–20), including pocket positions 7, 8 and 46. The multiple alignment (`-mm 4`) instead pairs 6UES like the CM and like 6UET. At these low TM-scores, a single pairwise alignment can be unstable, and the multiple alignment is more consistent.

**Multiple vs pairwise.** In the multiple alignment, the pocket pairs match the pairwise ones for every chain except 6UES, where they match the CM.

### Reading
- Structure-based alignment works and is consistent with the CM wherever the CM is confident.
- It **fixes the 2dG-I J1/2 register** and **recovers SAM-I/IV positions 102–105** that the CM leaves unmapped.
- Use the multiple alignment over the whole set rather than single pairwise runs, which are unstable at TM < 0.45.
- A per-position distance (or agreement between the two methods) remains the reliability measure.

**Method additions.**
- **Ligand moieties.** Contacts are split by SAM moiety, using atom names: adenine; ribose; S⁺ centre (C5′, SD, CE; in SFG, CD and NE); amino acid. The RNA side uses the `base_edges.py` rules.
- **Conformation.** χ is the O4′–C1′–N9–C4 torsion: syn if −30 to 120°, otherwise anti. N6–CA is the distance from the adenine N6 to the amino-acid Cα: short means folded, long means extended.
- **Sulfonium environment.** RNA N and O atoms within 4.0 Å of SD (CD in SFG) and of the methyl CE, using any contact type.
- **Bridges.**
  - **Water:** ligand N/O, then water within 3.5 Å, then RNA N/O within 3.5 Å.
  - **Metal:** fingeRNAt cutoffs (Mg 3.2, Na 3.6, K 3.9, others 3.5 Å).
  - **Coordinates:** from the ModelServer `residueSurroundings`, 7 Å, model 1.
- **ECIFP:** computed across all primary-site copies of all classes. It is position-free, so it is comparable across folds.

### Reading
- **Three recognition strategies for the sulfonium.**
  1. **SAM-I clan (I, IV, I/IV):** a folded, syn SAM with S⁺ and CH₃ against uracil O2 carbonyls (W/S corner) in the minor groove.
  2. **SAM-II/SAM-V:** an extended, anti SAM with S⁺ and CH₃ surrounded by three uracil O4 carbonyls (W/H corner) and an adenine N6.
  3. **SAM-III and SAM-VI:** no carbonyl cradle within 4 Å. SAM-III shows only O2′ and N7 near S⁺ and differs in conformation between SAM (syn, 7.9 Å) and SAH (anti, 10.8 Å). SAM-VI has nothing within 4 Å.
- **The SAM/SAH riboswitch reads the adenosine.**
  - The adenine and ribose contacts are complete in all 21 copies.
  - The amino acid is mostly unmodelled.
  - The sulfonium environment varies between copies.
  - Adenosine, AMP and MTA occupy the same site.
  - This is consistent with a riboswitch that does not discriminate SAM from SAH.
- **Adenine.** Every class contacts it through the Watson–Crick edge (or a W corner) plus stacking on the base face.
- **The fingerprint follows strategy, not Rfam family.**
  - SAM-I and SAM-I/IV score 0.92, and SAM-II and SAM-V 0.90.
  - Unrelated folds score 0.5–0.78.
  - SAM-IV's single cryo-EM copy (4.1 Å) scores lower against SAM-I (0.70), although its pocket contacts and S⁺ environment are SAM-I-like. Contact sets at that resolution are sparse.
- **Caveats.**
  - **Small numbers:** one copy for SAM-II, SAM-IV and SAM-V; two or three for SAM-III, SAM-VI, SAM-I/IV and SAH.
  - **Water and ion bridges** depend on resolution and refinement. The Ba²⁺ and Sr²⁺ are crystallisation ions.
  - **Distances are not interaction energies:** a 4 Å environment is a geometric proxy for electrostatic recognition.
